# Transfer 30-60-90 — Move 3

**This is not a worksheet.**  
It is the **outer half of the operator circuit** — the part that keeps running after Friday when the course scaffolding is gone.

```text
INNER CIRCUIT (Mon–Fri blocks)
  direct → harness → verify → adjudicate → bound → choose machine → prove transfer once

OUTER CIRCUIT (this file, living)
  carry method to real work → compound craft → govern under real constraints
```

At B0 you are choosing the target. Pick a workload that is real, recurring, and yours — something you will still be doing in March. Every seed after this lands on it, so a vague choice here costs you all week.

At P7 you are not inventing a plan. You are reading seven dated seeds and turning them into horizons. If a seed is empty, fill it before you draft. A horizon written over an empty seed is a commitment to a capability you never proved you had.

At P8 you are defending, not writing.

## Transfer-chat prompts

### B0 — open the file and name the target

```text
Transfer opening — outer circuit. Read operator/TRANSFER_30_60_90.md.

I have just finished B0: brief, mission, log, adversarial, measurement row.

Interview me in short turns:
1. What is on my desk? Role, and the 2-4 recurring workloads that are actually mine.
2. Push me to name ONE of them as the workload this week's seeds land on.
   Reject anything I would not still be doing in three months.
   Reject anything I cannot describe as a repeating product with a reader.
3. Write Identity from my answers, including the named workload.
4. Set Status to Living.

Do not draft horizons. Do not fill any seed section. One workload, named, is the whole job.
```

### P1–P7 — the dated seed line (in the block's own chat, after the measurement row)

```text
Seed line for operator/TRANSFER_30_60_90.md.

Block I just finished: [P1/P2/…].
The workload my seeds land on: [from Identity].

1. What did this block actually prove I can do — one sentence, no theater?
2. Turn it into one line for the "From [block]" seed section that is specific to MY
   workload, checkable, and bound. Challenge vagueness. Interview me if a field is empty.
3. Give me the finished seed rows with today's date in the Date field, ready to paste.

Do not open the horizons. Do not seal. I own every line.
```

### P7 — draft the horizons

```text
Transfer horizons. Read operator/TRANSFER_30_60_90.md.

All seed sections P1-P7 should now be filled and dated. Check them first:
name any that are empty or undated and make me fix those before we draft.

Then, from the seeds only:
1. Draft 30 / 60 / 90 outcomes — at most three per horizon, each observable in
   that horizon by someone who is not me.
2. Fill the 30-day rhythm, walls, bounds, and not-yet risks.
3. Attack every line that could survive without me doing anything differently.
4. Set Status to Draft horizons complete.

Do not seal. Do not invent a seed I did not write.
```

### P8 — seal

```text
Transfer seal. Read operator/TRANSFER_30_60_90.md and run the seal checklist adversarially.

Go item by item. For each one, quote the line in the file that satisfies it, or
tell me it fails. Do not pass an item on my say-so.

Attack specifically:
- Seed dates that cluster on one day.
- Outcomes that are not observable inside their horizon.
- Bounds that are aspirations rather than refusals.
- Any line that would read the same for any student in the room.

Only when every item holds: set Status to SEALED with today's date.
```

### Monthly (after the course)

```text
Transfer review. Read TRANSFER_30_60_90.md and recent operator logs.
What drifted? What check failed? What bound went soft?
Smallest revision to keep the outer circuit closed. I own accepts.
```
