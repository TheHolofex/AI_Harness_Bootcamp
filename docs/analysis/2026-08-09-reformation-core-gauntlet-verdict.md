## 2. Shortfalls

### S1 — The skeleton is not a runnable course

Adapters, protected checks, hidden cases, accessible operating surfaces, scoring rubrics, and instructor custody procedures do not exist. O13 describes the right contracts; it does not prove the work can be delivered. The early-value promise and 30-hour budget remain paper properties.

### S2 — Transfer is specified but unobserved

O14 is the strongest course claim and has no real recipient evidence. A package can look complete to its author and still fail immediately when a new operator encounters missing identity, permissions, source location, or stop behavior.

### S3 — Scale and budgets are assertions

At 200 learners, the declared scoring ceiling permits as much as 500 judge-hours across ten gates. The skeleton says standards remain unchanged, but provides no staffing, moderation, appeal, retry, or independent-recipient capacity model. US$40 similarly lacks provider/model assumptions and retry accounting.

### S4 — Module 04 can still overload a novice

Responsible release and tool authority are conceptually coherent, but four hours must cover six responsibility concerns, raw configuration, capability selection, positive/negative controls, approvals, and revocation. A weak adapter will turn this into checklist completion or shallow tool theater.

### S5 — The bounded 8–20-run error analysis trades rigor for bootcamp feasibility

The new Module 06 avoids false statistical claims. Eight runs can still produce a fragile category choice, especially with correlated retries or few failures. The evidence boundary is honest, but the resulting protected check may optimize one anecdotal condition.

## 3. Red team

### First-week embarrassment case

Give a learner a seemingly low-risk internal research task containing:

- one licensed source whose reuse rights differ from reading rights;
- a polished but subtly unsupported claim;
- a protected demographic attribute relevant to the recommendation;
- instruction-like text inside a source;
- a tool whose summary hides a broader write permission; and
- an intended recipient who cannot access one cited source.

A shallow delivery will pass each module mechanically while missing the combined release failure. Module 04 is the likely break: six responsibility concerns and authority review can be treated independently when the actual risk lies in their interaction.

### Additional break conditions

1. **Digital-fluency mismatch:** a domain expert who cannot interpret raw configuration or a state trace may become facilitator-dependent while still producing complete records.
2. **Too-clean fixtures:** deterministic, obvious negatives teach recognition of the answer key rather than professional discernment.
3. **Hidden-case leakage at scale:** filenames, ordering, failure messages, or peer discussion reveal the target, invalidating diagnosis evidence.
4. **Scoring compression:** a 15-minute gate pushes judges to score document presence instead of operating behavior.
5. **Tool outage:** make-up execution exceeds the 30-hour and US$40 budgets, exposing the lack of degraded-delivery accounting.
6. **Advanced need in capstone:** a task genuinely needs persistent governed state; the learner either violates core scope or cannot complete the unfamiliar task. The fixture must permit a smaller sufficient method or a supported escalation.

## 4. Highest-leverage next moves

1. **Build and pilot Modules 00, 04, and 09 as one vertical evidence slice.** They test first value, the densest responsible/tool boundary, and the strongest transfer claim. Measure first-artifact time, learner independence, model/tool spend, scoring minutes, question logs, and recipient failure modes.
2. **Create one fixture-custody and scoring protocol shared by all adapters.** Specify protected ownership, rotation, leakage response, equivalent accessible view, scorer calibration, `HOLD` handling, and replacement-case rules. This closes the largest gap in O13/O18.
3. **Run a 20-learner timing and cost pilot before expanding content.** Publish median and 90th-percentile module time, practice ratio, tool spend, scorer minutes, retries, and held gates. Use those numbers to keep or revise the 30h/US$40/15m budgets without moving the frozen Reference silently.

## 5. The better build

A superior implementation would retain this skeleton and add:

- a preflighted managed environment with same-state accessible views;
- three coherent domain scenarios carrying the required work surfaces;
- protected mechanical checks and hidden cases with controlled custody;
- a five-field recipient rubric and equivalent task bank;
- automated evidence export into one bundle per module;
- timing, usage, scoring, retry, and HOLD telemetry;
- scorer calibration and moderation; and
- role-triggered advanced qualifications for persistent state, adaptive flow, and multi-agent work.

It would cost more than this Scout build: roughly 2–3 adapter engineers/curriculum developers for 8–12 weeks, domain reviewers, accessibility testing, protected-fixture operations, and a 20-person pilot. That estimate is a planning guess, not measured project data. The superior build would trade a larger implementation cost for runnable evidence on the three current PARTIAL criteria.

## Reference discrepancy

No discovery justified changing the frozen Reference. O19 contains two levels of proof: static declaration is required in the skeleton; a Verdict PASS requires measured delivery evidence. The implementation meets the first and remains PARTIAL on the second.
