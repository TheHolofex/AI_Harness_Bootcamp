## 1. Summary

At approximately 1735Z on 15 June 2026 diesel was released on the fuel hardstanding at Bastion Reach (W-31) during a transfer from a road tanker into the site bulk tank. The release was stopped within about two minutes by the tanker driver operating the emergency shut-off at the cabinet. The spill was contained on the hardstanding and in the site drainage. Absorbent was deployed within ten minutes and booms were laid in the roadside ditch as a precaution the same evening. No one was injured. No product reached the river.

The tanker was not a group asset. It belonged to the Cordell Basin Relief Consortium and was on site under the standing fuel delivery arrangement. The cause was the failure of the tanker's own delivery hose.

## 4. Findings

4.1 The hose failed by a longitudinal split through the reinforcement, consistent with age and internal degradation rather than with impact, crushing or over-pressure. The retained sample shows no external damage at the point of failure.

4.2 The hose's inspection record shows it was last pressure tested in April 2025 and was therefore outside the consortium's own twelve-month test interval at the time of failure. The consortium accepts this.

4.3 The tanker was a consortium asset operated by a consortium driver. No group vehicle, no group equipment and no group personnel contributed to the failure.

4.4 The site response was correct and prompt. The emergency shut-off worked as intended. The spill kit was complete, in date and where it was supposed to be. Containment on the hardstanding held.

4.5 The interceptor performed within capacity and had been serviced within its interval. Nothing reached the river.

4.6 One weakness in the group's own arrangements was identified. The gate check for delivery vehicles verifies the driver, the load and the paperwork for the product, but it does not verify test currency for the vehicle's delivery equipment. Had it done so, this tanker would not have been permitted to connect.

4.7 An account has circulated locally, and appeared in the Selwyn Valley Bulletin of 11 August 2026, that a group tanker lost its load at Bastion Reach. That account is wrong. The safety cell has responded to the enquiry and the correction is on the record.

## 5. Root cause

Failure of an out-of-test delivery hose on a partner tanker.

## 6. Contributory factors

- The group's delivery gate check does not test the currency of the delivering vehicle's own equipment certification.
- The consortium's hose test register is held centrally and is not carried on the vehicle, so neither the driver nor the receiving site could have checked it at the point of delivery.

## 8. Learning to be passed to all detachments

The response worked because the kit was in the cabinet, the cabinet was where the sign said it was, and two people knew how to isolate without being told. That is not luck and it should not be allowed to decay. The failure that started it was in equipment the group did not own and did not check. Where a partner's equipment is connected to a group asset, the group is entitled to see the certificate, and from now on it will ask for it.
