## 1. Scope

This review covers driver duty length, rest and fatigue on both supply corridors over the second quarter of 2026. It considers when drivers start, how long they are on duty, how much of that duty is spent driving and how much waiting, and what drivers themselves report about tiredness.

It does not consider tasking, route selection, load or scheduling priority. Those are Movement Desk matters and are outside this review. Where a routing or timing arrangement appears below it is stated as a fact of the working day and nothing is inferred from it beyond its effect on the driver.

## 2. Method

- Duty records for 61 crews over the quarter, drawn from the movement log and the detachment duty boards.
- An anonymous fatigue questionnaire issued to all drivers on both corridors. Forty-seven returns from a possible seventy-one.
- Six ride-alongs by the author, four on AMBER and two on CINDER, all on ordinary working serials.
- Interviews with the two detachment transport sergeants and with the aid station.
- Review of near miss entries involving driver alertness in the period (NM-2026-020, NM-2026-028).

## 3. The shape of the working day on each corridor

Route AMBER runs north through Checkpoint 2 (W-19), the Marrow Cut, and over the Selwyn at the Selwyn Bridge (W-41). Checkpoint 2 passes no traffic between 2200Z and 0500Z under MEM-2026-041, which remains in force. AMBER is therefore worked as a daylight corridor, and serials are built so that they present at the checkpoint at or shortly after the 0500Z reopening.

Route CINDER runs south-west through Node D (W-07) and crosses the Selwyn by vehicle ferry at Ferry Point (W-22). There is no overnight restriction on CINDER, but there is no ferry outside sailing hours, and sailings are suspended by the operator when the river is up.

The two corridors therefore produce two quite different duty shapes, and they stress drivers in different ways.

## 4. Route AMBER: early starts and darkness

4.1 Four duties in five on AMBER begin before 0400Z. Crews are called at around 0300Z, marshal at the assembly lay-by from about 0330Z, and are on the road well before first light for most of the year.

4.2 This is the dominant fatigue exposure on the estate. It is not the length of the duty, which is long but not exceptional. It is the start time. A 0300Z call means a driver is trying to sleep from roughly 1900Z the previous evening, in accommodation shared with people who are not on that pattern, and in most cases with no meal since the middle of the previous afternoon.

4.3 Drivers report the low point at between two and three hours after first move, which on this pattern falls between 0630Z and 0730Z, in the run up through the Marrow Cut. Three of the four ride-alongs on AMBER observed at least one crew member asleep in the passenger seat on that stretch, which is permitted and desirable, and one observed a driver who by his own account was "not far off it".

4.4 Pre-dawn marshalling itself is a hazard separate from the driving. Crews are working around vehicles in darkness, on a lay-by with no fixed lighting, with engines running and reversing movements taking place. There has been no injury but the conditions are poor.

4.5 There is no hot food or drink available at the call time. The catering point opens at 0500Z. Every driver who commented on welfare in the questionnaire raised this, and several raised nothing else.

4.6 Back-to-back early starts are common. Nine drivers worked five or more consecutive duties starting before 0400Z during the quarter. Two worked eight consecutive. Self-reported fatigue scores rise sharply from the fourth consecutive early start and do not recover until two clear nights have been had.

## 5. Route CINDER: waiting

5.1 CINDER starts later, at a civilised hour, and involves less driving. It is not, however, a light duty, because the duty is stretched at the far end by waiting.

5.2 Waiting at the slips is unpredictable. Crews may be marshalled and away quickly or may sit for a long time, and they cannot tell in advance which it will be. Drivers wait in cabs, frequently with nothing to do, no facilities within reach and no reliable information about how long the wait will be.

5.3 Waiting of this kind is not restful. Nine questionnaire returns from CINDER drivers made the same point in different words: an hour sat in a cab at the slip is more tiring than an hour driving, and it does not count as a break because it cannot be relied on to last.

5.4 When the operator does not sail, the day is lost and the crew is either stood down late or re-tasked at short notice. Both are disruptive to the following day's rest.

5.5 Welfare at the slip is poor. There is one portable toilet, no shelter and no water point on the group's side of the marshalling area.

## 6. Self-reported fatigue

Scores are on the standing 1 to 7 scale, 1 being fully alert and 7 being fighting sleep. Drivers scored themselves at three points in the duty.

| Point in duty | AMBER median | AMBER worst quartile | CINDER median | CINDER worst quartile |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| At call | 3 | 5 | 2 | 3 |
| Two hours after first move | 4 | 6 | 2 | 4 |
| At end of duty | 4 | 5 | 4 | 6 |

The AMBER profile is the recognisable early-start profile: tired at the start, worst in the first hours of driving, partial recovery through the day. The CINDER profile is the recognisable long-duty profile: fresh at the start, degrading steadily, worst at the end, which is when the crew drives back.

## 7. Findings

7.1 Both corridors are worked within the group's duty limits. No breach of duty length was found in the quarter.

7.2 Compliance with limits is not the same as safety. The AMBER pattern is legal and tiring, and the tiredness is concentrated in the pre-dawn hours when the crews are least supported and least supervised.

7.3 The single most effective available intervention is not a change to hours. It is sleep opportunity before the duty, and welfare at the call time.

7.4 Consecutive early starts are the aggravating factor and are currently unmanaged. Nothing in the rostering process counts them.

7.5 Waiting time on CINDER is on-duty time and should be treated as such in fatigue terms, not discounted because the vehicle is stationary.

7.6 One driver has already self-reported as unfit to drive at the start of an early duty (NM-2026-028) and was stood down without difficulty or comment. That is the behaviour the group wants and it should be said so plainly and often.
