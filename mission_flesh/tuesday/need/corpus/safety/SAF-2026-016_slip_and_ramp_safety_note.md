# Safety Note: Working on the Ferry Slips at Ferry Point (W-22)

Reference: SAF-2026-016
Issued: 28 July 2026
Issued by: Safety Cell, Task Group MERIDIAN, Station Halberd
Agreed with: Selwyn Ferry Services Ltd (Dunphy, M., ferry operations manager)
To: All personnel tasked to Ferry Point (W-22), whether as crew, marshal, escort or visitor
Applies to: the upper slip, the lower slip, the ramp and the marshalling area below the kiosk
Supersedes: nil
Review: July 2027

**Classification:** TRAINING SYNTHETIC - EXERCISE NORTHWIND SHELF. All units, organisations, people, places and events in this document are invented.

---

## 1. Background

Three near misses at Ferry Point this year have been on or beside the slips: a marshal who slipped on the upper slip, a vehicle that rolled forward on the lower slip after the driver got out, and a mooring line that snatched. Nobody has been hurt. The slips are a sloping, wet, moving-vehicle environment beside deep water, and they deserve to be treated as one.

This note sets out how the group works on the slips. Selwyn Ferry Services own the structure and their master and crew have the final word on everything afloat and on the ramp. Where their instruction differs from this note, follow theirs and tell the safety cell afterwards.

## 2. Footwear

2.1 Safety footwear with a cleated slip-resistant sole is required by anyone stepping onto either slip. Smooth-soled boots, worn-down soles and any footwear whose tread pattern has closed up are not acceptable on the slip and will be turned back at the top.

2.2 Check your own soles before you go down. A boot that is fine on a hardstanding is not necessarily fine on a wet slip at a gradient.

2.3 Spare boots in common sizes are held at the detachment stores. There is no reason to work on the slip in the wrong footwear.

## 3. The slip surface

3.1 Both slips grow algae and weed on the lower parts that are regularly wetted. The operator washes the slips down on a schedule and after any period out of service. Where the growth has come back, the affected band is coned off until it has been cleared.

3.2 The band between wet and dry is where people go over. It looks like the dry part and behaves like the wet part. Cross it deliberately, with your weight over your feet, and do not carry anything two-handed across it.

3.3 Do not walk down the slip when you can walk down the steps at the side. The steps have a handrail. The slip does not.

3.4 Report a slippery patch to the ferry crew at the time. Do not wait until you are back at the top and have forgotten.

## 4. Working near the water

4.1 A lifejacket is worn by anyone working within 3 metres of the water edge, anyone handling lines, and anyone on the ramp while it is being raised or lowered. Lifejackets are held in the kiosk and at the group's marshalling point.

4.2 Two throwing lines and two lifebuoys are sited on the upper slip. Know where they are before you need them. Check on arrival that they are there and serviceable, and report it if they are not.

4.3 Nobody works alone on either slip at any time. This applies to a five-minute errand as much as to a shift.

4.4 The water is cold enough year round to take the breath out of somebody who goes in. Anyone entering the water is a casualty from the moment they enter it, whether or not they are swimming, and the response is a throwing line and the ferry crew, not a second person in the water.

4.5 Edge protection on the upper slip is the operator's chain barrier. It is a demarcation, not a guardrail, and it will not hold a person's weight. Do not lean on it and do not sit on it.

## 5. Vehicles on the slip

5.1 Drivers remain in the cab, with the engine running and the vehicle in gear or park, from joining the queue until marshalled onto the ramp. Nobody gets out on the slip to stretch, to look, or to talk to the next vehicle.

5.2 Chocks are carried and used on any vehicle that stops on the slip for any reason.

5.3 Marshals stand where the driver can see them and never between a vehicle and the water, and never between two vehicles.

5.4 Hand signals only. Nothing shouted at the slip is audible over the ramp and the engines, and a driver who cannot hear you will guess.

5.5 Reversing on the slip is a banksman task without exception.

## 6. Weather and river

6.1 The operator suspends sailings when the river is up. That call belongs to the ferry master and the group does not question it, argue it or ask for it to be reconsidered. When it is made, the marshalling area is cleared and vehicles are turned at the top of the lane, not on the slip.

6.2 In wind, work on the slip stops if the ferry crew say it stops.

6.3 In darkness the slip is worked only under the fixed lighting. If a light is out, the affected part of the slip is out of use until it is replaced.

6.4 After heavy rain the slips are treated as slippery whether or not they look it, and the coned band is widened.

## 7. Controls at a glance

| Hazard | Control |
| --- | --- |
| Slipping on a wet or weeded slip | Cleated safety footwear; coned band over growth; use the stepped access; wash-down schedule |
| Falling into the water | Lifejacket within 3 m of the edge and when handling lines; no lone working; throwing lines and lifebuoys sited and checked |
| Vehicle rolling on the gradient | Driver stays in cab; engine running and vehicle secured; chocks on any stopped vehicle |
| Being struck by a vehicle | Marshal in the driver's view, never between vehicle and water; hand signals; banksman for all reversing |
| Line snatch | Standing positions marked; nobody in the bight; ferry crew handle lines wherever possible |
| Ramp movement | Nobody on the ramp while it moves; lifejacket if working near it |
| Cold water shock | Treat any immersion as a casualty; line and crew, not a second person in |

## 8. Action

Detachment commanders are to brief this note to everyone currently tasked to Ferry Point and to include it in the arrival brief for anyone tasked in future. Return a one-line confirmation to the safety cell by 15 August 2026.
