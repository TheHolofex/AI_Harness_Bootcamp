## 5. Commentary on the backlog

a. **The backlog is growing, slowly and steadily.** It has risen in five of the
   six months of the period and now stands at 58 requests against 26 at the end
   of January. The section is closing roughly as many requests as it raises in a
   normal month and losing ground in a heavy one. Nothing in the trend suggests
   it will correct itself.

b. **Surfacing is the largest single group and the slowest moving.** Seventeen
   surfacing requests are outstanding, most of them on the open road stretches
   of both corridors and on the Node D hardstanding. Surfacing work needs a
   possession of the running surface, and possessions on Route AMBER can only be
   worked inside the daylight window because Checkpoint 2 passes no traffic
   between 2200Z and 0500Z under MEM-2026-041. The result is that surfacing
   competes directly with movement serials for the same hours, and the Movement
   Desk has first call. Six of the seventeen have been deferred at least twice
   for this reason.

c. **Hardstanding at Node D is the worst individual asset.** Seven outstanding
   requests, median close time of 34 days, and joint failures reopening within
   weeks of patching. The section assesses that continued patching is money
   thrown away and that the area needs relaying as a planned task rather than a
   reactive one. A case is being prepared for the next budget round. Estimated
   cost of a relay is 84,000 exercise credits against roughly 11,000 spent on
   patching since January.

d. **Structures.** Seven requests raised, only two closed, median close time 61
   days. This group is small in number and large in effect, because a structural
   request cannot be closed in house and depends on Ardwick Structural attending
   with the right trade. Of the five outstanding, three are parapet and handrail
   items on minor culvert crossings and two are approach kerbing. The Selwyn
   Bridge stringer and deck plate works are not counted in this table: they sit
   on their own contract line, they are outstanding, and they await call-off.

e. **Ferry Point approach road** produced eight outstanding requests, all
   surfacing or drainage on the approach and the marshalling area. The approach
   takes heavy standing loads from serials waiting for a slot, and the edges are
   breaking up where vehicles pull over. Edge strengthening on the outer two
   metres would remove most of these requests and is recommended.

f. **Fuel installations** show a long median close time on a small number of
   requests. This is a lead time problem rather than a capacity problem. Bexley
   Fuel Systems will not attend for a single item and batches its visits, so a
   minor fault sits until there are three of them.

g. **What is working.** Drainage, signage, catering, water and internal
   electrical are all closing inside two weeks and none carries a meaningful
   backlog. The quarterly culvert clearance introduced in March has visibly
   reduced the number of reactive drainage requests: April, May and June
   together produced fewer than January and February.

## 6. Recommendations

1. Programme the Node D hardstanding relay as a planned task in the next budget
   round and stop reactive patching there.
2. Fund edge strengthening on the Ferry Point approach road.
3. Agree a standing possession window with the Movement Desk for open road
   surfacing on both corridors, so that surfacing requests are not competing
   week by week for the same hours.
4. Batch fuel installation requests deliberately rather than by accident, and
   set a call-out threshold with Bexley Fuel Systems.
5. Review the structures group monthly rather than quarterly. It is small enough
   to be forgotten and slow enough to matter.
