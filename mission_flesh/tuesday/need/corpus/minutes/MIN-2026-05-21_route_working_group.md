## 4. Ferry charter administration

4.1 Dunphy, M. joined by telephone. The Chair thanked him for attending and said the purpose of the item was administrative rather than operational: the Group was content with the service and wished to tidy the paperwork.

4.2 **The charter.** Bergstrom, A. summarised. Charter CHT-2025-017 was let to Selwyn Ferry Services Ltd on 24 September 2025 and commenced on 1 October 2025 for a term of twelve months, with an option to extend by six months exercisable by MERIDIAN on sixty days' notice. The vessel named in the charter is MV Tolland Carrier. The operator provides master, crew, fuel, insurance and shore staff. MERIDIAN provides marshalling, traffic control on the approaches and lighting of the marshalling area.

4.3 **Charge structure.** The charter provides for:

| Element | Basis | Rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum monthly charge | Payable each calendar month whether or not the service is called | 46,500 credits |
| Operating day | Each day the service is made available and worked, credited against the minimum | 2,150 credits |
| Standby day | Each day the service is made available but not worked at MERIDIAN's election | 900 credits |
| Crew overtime | Per crew hour outside the scheduled operating window | 74 credits |
| Shore staff | Included | Nil |

4.4 Sandoval, E. reported that in every month since commencement the accumulated operating and standby charges had fallen short of the minimum monthly charge, so MERIDIAN had in effect paid the minimum and received the service. The shortfall in the last three months averaged some 9,700 credits per month. He said the arrangement was not necessarily bad value, since the minimum buys availability, but that the Group should be aware that the marginal charges are academic at current levels of use.

4.5 **Administrative faults.** Bergstrom, A. listed the problems in the invoicing.

(a) Invoices arrive without the supporting daily log. The charter requires the log to be attached. Three invoices have been paid without it because the alternative was to hold payment.

(b) Days on which the operator suspended the service because the river was up have been billed variously as standby days and as operating days. The charter does not deal with an operator-initiated suspension at all. It deals only with a day not worked at MERIDIAN's election, which is a different thing.

(c) The crew overtime line has been billed in whole hours where the charter says per hour worked; the operator's practice is to round up.

(d) One invoice carried a fuel surcharge for which there is no provision in the charter. The operator has withdrawn it.

4.6 Dunphy, M. accepted (a), (c) and (d) without argument and said the daily log would be attached from the next invoice and that overtime would be billed to the quarter hour. On (b) he said that a suspension day still costs the operator a crewed vessel and shore staff and that his company's view was that it should attract the standby rate. He accepted that the charter was silent and suggested a written variation rather than a continuing argument invoice by invoice.

4.7 The Chair said that was a sensible position and that MERIDIAN would not seek to recover past sums, but that the treatment must be settled prospectively. Bergstrom, A. to draft a variation providing that a day on which the operator suspends the service is billed at the standby rate, and that the operator notifies the suspension to the Movement Desk by telephone and confirms by signal in the manner already recorded in the constraints register.

4.8 Marchetti, D. asked that the variation also record that the operator's judgement on suspension is final and is not to be argued with at the slipway. Ingersoll, P., the ferry master, had reported one occasion on which a driver had pressed him to sail. Radcliffe, J. said the Provost would deal with any repetition. The Chair directed that the point be included in the driver's brief for Route CINDER.

4.9 Dunphy, M. raised two minor matters. The slipway lighting on the north bank is on a timeclock that does not follow the change in daylight, and the marshalling area at Ferry Point holds water after rain, which makes the approach greasy. Petrakis, V. said the drainage scheme was in the works programme and that he would look at the timeclock as a small works item. Dunphy, M. left the meeting.

## 5. Consortium traffic on the crossing

5.1 Adeyemi, F. joined for this item.

5.2 Bergstrom, A. explained that consortium vehicles use the crossing under a side letter of 14 November 2025 rather than under any contract with the operator. The consortium presents its vehicles at the marshalling area, MERIDIAN marshals them with its own, and MERIDIAN recharges the consortium a fixed contribution of 6,400 credits per month towards the minimum monthly charge.

5.3 Sandoval, E. said the contribution was set when the side letter was written and had not been revisited. It bears no relation to the consortium's actual use, which the Group has no reliable measure of, because the marshalling record does not distinguish reliably between MERIDIAN and consortium vehicles.

5.4 Adeyemi, F. said the consortium was content with a fixed contribution and would prefer it to a measured one, as it made budgeting simple. He asked only that the figure be stable for the remainder of the charter term.

5.5 Loughrey, K. raised priority. Where both parties present vehicles for the same sailing period and the vessel cannot take them all, there is no written rule and the matter is settled at the slipway by whoever is marshalling. He said this worked, but only because the marshallers are sensible, and that it would not survive a busy morning and a new corporal.

5.6 The Chair agreed. He directed that a priority rule be written into the driver's brief and the side letter: MERIDIAN serials carrying medical or fuel loads take precedence, then consortium vehicles carrying relief loads, then all other traffic in order of presentation. Adeyemi, F. said the consortium would accept that.

5.7 Marchetti, D. asked that the marshalling record be improved so that the two parties' vehicles are distinguishable, whatever charging basis is used, because at present the file cannot answer a simple question about who used the crossing. Agreed.

5.8 Insurance was raised. Bergstrom, A. confirmed that the operator's cover extends to vehicles on the vessel irrespective of owner, and that the side letter records that the consortium carries its own cover for its vehicles and personnel ashore. No action.

5.9 Adeyemi, F. withdrew.
