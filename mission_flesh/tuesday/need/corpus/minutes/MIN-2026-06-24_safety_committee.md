## 5. Manual handling

5.1 Quinlan, T. introduced the item. Manual handling accounts for the largest single share of minor injuries and near misses across the last three quarters, and the pattern is consistent: stores handling in confined lock-ups, stacking above shoulder height, and repeated lifting of ration and water containers.

5.2 Tan, W. said that Supply had two constraints. First, the lock-ups at Node B and Bastion Reach are small and staff stack high because there is no floor area. Second, the sack barrows held are unsuitable for the lock-up thresholds, which are stepped.

5.3 The Committee agreed the following.

(a) A stacking height limit of shoulder height for any item handled manually, to be marked on the lock-up wall with a painted line at each site.

(b) Procurement of four stair-climbing sack barrows suitable for stepped thresholds, estimated at 1,150 credits in total. Tan, W. to raise the demand.

(c) Manual handling refresher training for all stores staff and for vehicle crews who load their own vehicles. Ashby, P. said the training team could deliver this in four sessions. Sixty-one personnel are in scope.

(d) Two-person lift to be mandatory for water containers above half capacity.

5.4 Halloran, M. asked that the Aid Station be told of any back complaint even where no treatment is sought, so that the pattern can be tracked. Agreed and to be included in the safety instruction.

5.5 The Chair said he did not want this to become a paper exercise and asked Quinlan, T. to sample-check the painted lines and the stacking at each site during his next round of visits.

## 6. Fuel spill, Bastion Reach, 12 June 2026 (NM/26/026)

6.1 Mott, R. gave the account. At approximately 1420Z on 12 June a bulk fuel delivery was in progress at the Bastion Reach fuel point. The delivery was made by a road tanker belonging to the Cordell Basin Relief Consortium under the shared fuel arrangement. During the transfer a coupling on the tanker's discharge hose parted at the camlock and diesel discharged onto the hardstanding for a period estimated at fifteen to twenty seconds before the tanker driver operated the emergency stop.

6.2 The quantity lost is estimated at 45 litres. The whole of the spill was retained within the fuel point bund, which had been repaired in March under EWB/26/04. Mott, R. said plainly that had the spill occurred before the bund repair a proportion of it would have reached the surface water drain. The Committee recorded that the bund repair had earned its cost within three months.

6.3 Clean-up was by absorbent granulate from the site spill kit, followed by a second application from the reserve kit held at the Aid Station, the site kit having been insufficient. Contaminated absorbent was drummed and disposed of through the waste contractor on 15 June. No fuel reached the drainage system and no environmental report was required.

6.4 **Cause.** Cpl Vasquez, R. reported that the camlock arms on the tanker's hose had been secured but that one arm was worn and did not seat fully. The tanker was withdrawn from service by the consortium the same day. The consortium's own inspection found the same wear on two further hoses in its fleet.

6.5 **Findings.** The Committee found as follows.

(a) The immediate cause was a defective coupling on a partner vehicle, outside MERIDIAN's inspection regime.

(b) The contributory cause was that no MERIDIAN person was standing at the fuel point during a third-party delivery. The delivery was left to the tanker driver and the site was unattended at the point of failure.

(c) The site spill kit was of insufficient capacity for a spill of this size, which is not a large spill.

(d) The response was otherwise sound and the bund performed as designed.

6.6 **Actions agreed.**

(a) A MERIDIAN attendant is to be present throughout any third-party fuel delivery, at any site, with authority to stop the transfer. Mott, R. to issue.

(b) Spill kit scales at all fuel points to be reviewed and increased. Mott, R. and Tan, W.

(c) The consortium to be asked to confirm in writing that its hose couplings have been inspected across the fleet and to provide its inspection interval. Marchetti, D. in his absence, through the Chair.

(d) An extract of this item to be released to the consortium.

6.7 Radcliffe, J. asked whether the incident should be treated as a chargeable loss against the consortium. Quinlan, T. said the quantity was trivial and that pursuing it would cost more than the fuel. The Chair agreed and directed that it be written off, with the safety points pursued instead.

## 7. Driving hours

7.1 Loughrey, K. reported on compliance with driving and duty hour limits for the quarter. The limits in force are a maximum of ten hours driving in a duty period, a maximum of thirteen hours on duty, a break of not less than forty-five minutes after four and a half hours of driving, and a minimum of eleven hours rest between duty periods.

7.2 Two exceedances were recorded in the quarter, both of the duty hour limit and neither of the driving hour limit.

(a) On 21 April a crew on Route CINDER exceeded the duty limit by fifty minutes after a suspension of the ferry crossing when the river was up. The crew was held at Ferry Point and then returned to Station Halberd rather than being accommodated forward. Loughrey, K. said the decision to return was wrong and that the crew should have been laid up. A standing instruction now provides for lay-up at Ferry Point in that circumstance.

(b) On 9 June a crew exceeded the duty limit by twenty-five minutes as a result of a vehicle defect on return to Node D. No fault was found with the crew or the tasking.

7.3 Loughrey, K. said that compliance on Route AMBER had been easier to hold since the corridor became a daylight corridor. Checkpoint 2 passes no traffic between 2200Z and 0500Z under MEM-2026-041, so a serial cannot be extended into the night to make up time. The Movement Desk applies a hard release cut-off and a serial not released by it is replanned as a lay-up or cancelled. The effect is that the schedule itself now enforces the hours rather than the squadron having to.

7.4 Ashby, P. raised the welfare consequence of lay-ups, which fall on crews at short notice. He asked that a lay-up pack of bedding, rations and washing kit be held forward at both lay-up locations rather than carried. Tan, W. undertook to scale and hold it.

7.5 The Chair asked whether fatigue reporting was honest. Loughrey, K. said crews reported fatigue readily and that no one had been penalised for it. Ashby, P. supported this.
