## 1. Welcome and record

1.1 The Chair welcomed the consortium party. The minutes of the previous joint meeting of 23 April 2026 were agreed as a correct record.

1.2 Adeyemi, F. asked that the joint action list be carried at the front of the minutes in future rather than the back. Agreed.

## 2. Matters arising

2.1 **Hose coupling inspection (SAF/26/22).** Adeyemi, F. confirmed in writing at the meeting that the consortium had inspected hose couplings across its fleet following the spill at Bastion Reach on 12 June, that three hoses had been replaced, and that its inspection interval is now monthly with a record held at its transport office. The Chair thanked him and closed the action.

2.2 **Constraints register.** The consortium confirmed receipt of the current redacted register. Oyelaran, S. said the register was useful and asked to be told when it changes rather than having to ask. Agreed, the Secretariat will notify on reissue.

2.3 **Route briefing.** Iyer, S. confirmed that consortium drivers had been briefed on the current route cards. Route AMBER is worked as a daylight corridor, Checkpoint 2 passing no traffic between 2200Z and 0500Z under MEM-2026-041, and consortium movements on that corridor are planned accordingly. Oyelaran, S. said this had settled into routine and caused his organisation no difficulty. On Route CINDER the crossing priority rule agreed in May is in the driver's brief and has been applied without dispute.

## 3. Shared use of Node D (W-07)

3.1 Oyelaran, S. said the consortium's use of Node D had increased over the summer, chiefly in the morning period, and that queuing at the fuel point had been noticeable in June but less so in July.

3.2 Tan, W. explained the change: MERIDIAN vehicles now refuel on return in the evening rather than on departure, which has taken roughly half the demand out of the morning peak. He invited the consortium to adopt the same discipline for its own vehicles. Oyelaran, S. said he would try it and report.

3.3 **Marshalling lane.** The Chair explained the interim one-way working introduced after two near misses, and that the permanent scheme with a western exit was considered by the Estate Works Board in May and not funded this year. Oyelaran, S. said consortium drivers had found the interim arrangement confusing in its first fortnight because the signage went up before the briefing went out. Tan, W. accepted the point and undertook that any future change of traffic arrangement at a shared site would be notified to the consortium seven days before it takes effect.

3.4 **Bay condition.** Bays 24 to 31 remain restricted to light vehicles pending the hardstanding repair, which is now placed and awaiting a start. Adeyemi, F. asked whether the restriction affected the bays licensed to the consortium. Tan, W. confirmed that four of the eighteen licensed bays are within the restricted range.

3.5 **Hire agreement.** Sandoval, E. reported that the review of the hire agreement directed by the Route Working Group had been completed and issued as FIN-2026-008, and that a copy had been sent to Kirkbride, N. The recommendation is to retain a fixed charge with a modest reduction reflecting the restricted bays, and to serve no notice to terminate. Adeyemi, F. said the consortium would respond formally within the month and that his first reaction was favourable.

3.6 **Perimeter fence.** The southern perimeter fence repair has been placed against the term arrangement. The temporary barrier remains until it is done.

## 4. Waste

4.1 Strand, I. reported that the shared waste compound at Node D is regularly over-full by the second of the two weekly collections, and that the compound at Station Halberd is at capacity. The compound extension at Station Halberd was proposed to the Estate Works Board in February and was not programmed.

4.2 The Chair said the immediate question was whether the problem was volume or discipline. Strand, I. said both. Volume has risen with the level of activity, and segregation is poor: recyclable packaging, general waste and food waste all go into the same skips, which means the general waste skip fills first and everything else follows it.

4.3 Oyelaran, S. said the consortium had its own segregation policy and would enforce it on its own personnel, but that the compound had no separate containers to segregate into. Strand, I. accepted that.

4.4 Agreed:

(a) Three additional containers to be placed at Node D, marked for general waste, packaging and food waste, with pictorial signage in the languages in use at the site. Strand, I. to arrange, estimated 2,400 credits for hire and signage for the remainder of the year.

(b) Collection at Node D to increase from twice to three times weekly for a trial period of eight weeks, after which the frequency will be reviewed against actual fill. Additional cost 1,900 credits over the trial.

(c) Both parties to brief their personnel on segregation before the containers arrive, not after.

(d) The cost of waste at shared sites to be recharged to the consortium at the fixed proportion already used for other shared services, rather than measured. Kirkbride, N. agreed.

4.5 Kirkbride, N. asked whether clinical waste from the consortium's medical activity could go through the same route. Strand, I. said it could not and that clinical waste must go through the Aid Station's contracted route. Halloran, M. confirmed she could accommodate the consortium's volume. Agreed, with a separate written arrangement to follow.

## 5. Catering

5.1 Nwosu, C. reported that consortium personnel take meals at the Station Halberd dining facility and at Node D under the standing arrangement, at a charge per meal recovered monthly.

5.2 Two problems were raised.

(a) **Numbers.** The consortium does not notify numbers in advance, so catering caters for a standing estimate. In July the estimate was exceeded on nine days and short on eleven. Nwosu, C. said over-catering wastes food and under-catering means people go without. He asked for a daily figure by 1400Z for the following day.

(b) **Hours.** Consortium personnel arriving from the field after the evening service closes have been served from stores rather than from the line, which is untidy and unrecorded. Nwosu, C. proposed a late meal held back for a notified number, on the same daily notification.

5.3 Oyelaran, S. accepted both and said his duty officer would send the figure daily. Kirkbride, N. asked that the meal charge be confirmed in writing as unchanged for the remainder of the year. Sandoval, E. confirmed it.

5.4 Halloran, M. asked that the dietary and allergy information for consortium personnel be given to catering, as at present it is held only by the consortium. Kirkbride, N. said this raised a data question for his organisation and undertook to come back at the next meeting rather than agree it at the table. Noted.

## 6. Medical cover

6.1 Halloran, M. set out the current position. The MERIDIAN Aid Station treats consortium personnel for injury and acute illness arising in the field on the same basis as its own personnel. Continuing care, routine consultation and occupational health remain the consortium's responsibility.

6.2 Haverford, G. said the arrangement worked well in practice and that the consortium had no complaint about the standard of care. He raised three matters.

(a) **Records.** The Aid Station records a treatment episode but the record does not reach the consortium, so its own medical file for a person is incomplete. He asked for a copy of the treatment note to be released to the consortium medical lead with the patient's consent.

(b) **Evacuation.** Where a casualty requires evacuation beyond the Aid Station, the decision and the means are MERIDIAN's. Haverford, G. asked to be told at the time rather than afterwards.

(c) **First aid at consortium sites.** The consortium holds first aid cover at its own sites but its trained numbers have fallen through turnover. He asked whether consortium staff could attend MERIDIAN first aid training.

6.3 Halloran, M. agreed (a) subject to a consent form being signed at the point of treatment, and undertook to draft the form. She agreed (b) without qualification and will add the consortium medical lead to the notification list. On (c) she said places were available on the next two courses and offered six places in total.

6.4 Haverford, G. also asked about medical cover for lay-up serials north of Checkpoint 2 during the closed period, when the forward party cannot reach them. Halloran, M. explained that lay-up serials carry an enhanced first aid scale for this reason and offered to brief the consortium on the scale so that its own vehicles can carry the equivalent. Agreed.

6.5 The Chair asked whether there was any question of charging for treatment. Sandoval, E. said no charge is raised and none is proposed.

## 7. Any other business

7.1 Adeyemi, F. reported that the consortium would host the next meeting at its Cordell Basin office and would provide transport.

7.2 Kirkbride, N. asked for a single point of contact for invoicing queries. Sandoval, E. gave his own desk.

7.3 Iyer, S. had no business.

7.4 Prentice, H. had no business.

## 8. Date of next meeting

8.1 28 October 2026, 1030Z, Cordell Basin office. The consortium chairs.

The Chair closed the meeting at 1250Z.
