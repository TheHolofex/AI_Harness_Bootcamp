## 4. Node D staging

4.1 The Chair introduced the item. Route CINDER now carries load that was previously scheduled on AMBER, and Node D (W-07) is the pinch point on that corridor. The Group was asked to look at the staging arrangement as a whole.

4.2 **Layout.** Okonjo, B. described the site. The hardstanding is laid out as thirty-one bays in three ranks, with a marshalling lane along the northern edge and a single entry and exit at the eastern end. Circulation is two-way in the marshalling lane, which requires vehicles to reverse into bays in the middle rank.

4.3 Loughrey, K. said that two-way circulation in the marshalling lane was the source of most of the delay at the morning peak, and that a one-way circuit with a second exit at the western end would remove the reversing manoeuvre for the middle rank. Okonjo, B. said that a western exit would require a section of new hardstanding across the verge and a gate, and that he could not cost it from the meeting. Action to produce an estimate.

4.4 **Bay condition.** Surface break-up in the eastern bays has worsened since February. Bays 24 to 31 are now used only by light vehicles. The patch repair scheme is in the works programme and at tender. Petrakis, V. said he expected award in May.

4.5 **Driver facilities.** Tan, W. reported that the driver rest area at Node D consists of two containers, one of which has no working heater. Latrine provision is adequate for the current pattern but is at capacity at the morning peak. Waste is collected twice weekly and the compound is generally over-full by the second collection. The Chair asked that waste be raised with the consortium at the July coordination meeting, as consortium vehicles use the same compound.

4.6 **Lighting.** Petrakis, V. said that lighting at Node D was adequate. The Node B proposal at RWG/26/018 does not extend to Node D and no case is made for it.

4.7 **Security.** Radcliffe, J. reported that the perimeter fence on the southern side has two sections of collapsed post and that a temporary barrier is in place. This is not in the works programme and he asked that it be added. Petrakis, V. to raise at the Estate Works Board.

## 5. Node D fuel point throughput

5.1 Mott, R. joined for this item.

5.2 The fuel point has two dispensing positions served from a single bulk tank. Mean fill time across the last four weeks was seven minutes per vehicle including hose handling and documentation. The point is manned from 0500Z to 1900Z.

5.3 The problem is concentration rather than capacity. Serials release from Node D in a narrow window after first light, and effectively the whole of the day's demand presents between 0545Z and 0730Z. Outside that window the point is idle for long periods. Mott, R. said that a third dispensing position would help the peak but would be poor value across the day.

5.4 Loughrey, K. proposed instead that half the daily demand be moved to the previous evening, with vehicles fuelled on return rather than on departure. Mott, R. said this was practicable within the current manning, would require no capital work, and would need the discipline to be written into squadron standing orders rather than left to crews.

5.5 Agreed. Loughrey, K. to amend squadron standing orders so that vehicles returning to Node D refuel before parking, and Mott, R. to report the effect at the June meeting.

5.6 Mott, R. also reported that bulk deliveries to Node D are made three times a week and that the delivery vehicle has to reverse the length of the marshalling lane, which would be resolved by the one-way circuit at 4.3. Noted in support of the estimate.

5.7 Sandoval, E. asked whether the change would alter the fuel planning rate. Mott, R. said it would not; the same fuel is issued at a different time of day.

## 6. Hardstanding hire terms

6.1 Adeyemi, F. joined for this item.

6.2 Sandoval, E. set out the current arrangement. Under a hire agreement dated 3 September 2025, the Cordell Basin Relief Consortium has licensed use of eighteen bays at Node D at a charge of 340 credits per bay per month, payable monthly in arrears whether or not the bay is occupied. The agreement runs to 2 September 2026 and rolls on for a further twelve months unless notice is given.

6.3 Sandoval, E. reported that measured occupancy has run well below eighteen bays for most of the year, and that the consortium had asked informally whether the charge could be put on a usage basis. He said that a usage basis would transfer the risk of idle bays from the consortium to MERIDIAN and that he would not recommend it without a floor.

6.4 Adeyemi, F. said that the consortium's position was that it was paying for bays it did not use, and that in a month with light traffic the charge was hard to justify to its own finance committee. He accepted that MERIDIAN needed certainty of income against the maintenance liability.

6.5 Loughrey, K. observed that the idle bays were not in practice available to MERIDIAN, since they are licensed, and that if the eastern bays remain restricted to light vehicles the site is tighter than the bay count suggests.

6.6 The Chair said the Group could not settle a commercial term and referred the matter to Finance for a written review, to be brought back to the Group with a recommendation. Sandoval, E. undertook to produce the review before the summer.

6.7 Adeyemi, F. thanked the Group and withdrew.
