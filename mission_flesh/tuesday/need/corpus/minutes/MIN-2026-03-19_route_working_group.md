## 4. Route AMBER - Checkpoint 2 overnight closure

4.1 The Chair said that this was the substantial item of the meeting and that he had asked Deacon, R. to join by telephone.

4.2 **The measure.** Iyer, S. reported that MEM-2026-041 had been issued and is in force. Checkpoint 2 (W-19) passes no traffic between 2200Z and 0500Z. There is no provision in the memorandum for a serial to be passed inside the closed period, and no local discretion is delegated to the detachment. The Movement Desk has therefore restructured Route AMBER as a daylight corridor, and all AMBER planning from the current tasking cycle onward assumes that no serial is on the corridor north of Station Halberd during the closed period.

4.3 Deacon, R. confirmed the position from the checkpoint. The barrier goes down at 2200Z and does not lift until 0500Z. His detachment had turned no vehicle away since the measure took effect, which he attributed to the Movement Desk having reworked the schedule promptly rather than to any slackness in the measure. He asked that the Group note that his detachment has no authority to make an exception and that requests to do so, if any are made, will be refused.

4.4 **Effect on scheduling.** Iyer, S. took the Group through the revised AMBER pattern.

(a) The binding calculation is the last safe departure from Station Halberd. A serial must clear Checkpoint 2 northbound, run the Marrow Cut, cross the Selwyn Bridge, turn round and be back through Checkpoint 2 southbound, all before 2200Z. Working back from that, and allowing the standard turn-round at the bridge and the standard hold at Node B, the last release from Station Halberd for a same-day return serial is 1130Z.

(b) A serial released after 1130Z cannot return the same day. Such serials are planned as overnight lay-ups north of the checkpoint, using the hardstanding at the Selwyn Bridge approach, and return in the following daylight window.

(c) The first release of the day is at 0530Z, which allows the checkpoint to clear its opening routine at 0500Z and gives the serial a settled crossing.

(d) The practical consequence is that AMBER now supports either one long serial or two short serials in a day, where previously the schedule was built around a rolling pattern across the twenty-four hours.

4.5 **Discussion.** The following points were made.

(a) Loughrey, K. said that the change had bitten hardest on driver utilisation. Crews previously released late in the day now start early and finish early, and the squadron has had to rebuild the duty roster around a single daylight shift. He asked that the Group note the roster change as settled and not revisit it monthly.

(b) Loughrey, K. further reported that driving hours had not been breached under the new pattern, and that if anything the pattern was easier to police, because a serial cannot be extended into the night to make up lost time. The Chair said this was a real benefit and asked that it be recorded.

(c) Radcliffe, J. said that escort tasking had simplified. Escorts are now found for daylight serials only on AMBER, which has released escort capacity that had previously been held for night moves. Some of that capacity has been reassigned to Route CINDER. Sandoval, E. asked whether this produced a saving against the escort planning rate; Radcliffe, J. said it produced a reallocation rather than a saving, as the hours are still worked.

(d) Achebe, N. reported that the radio watch at the northern relay had been reduced to daylight hours plus a one-hour margin either side, with a listening watch overnight. This has released two signallers per day for other duties. He asked that any future change to AMBER timings be given to Signals at least seventy-two hours before it takes effect, so that the watch roster can be adjusted. Agreed.

(e) Halloran, M. said that medical cover on the corridor had been reduced correspondingly, and that the Aid Station now holds a daylight forward party rather than a twenty-four hour one. She noted that a serial laying up overnight north of the checkpoint is beyond the reach of the forward party until the checkpoint opens, and asked that lay-up serials carry an enhanced first aid scale. Agreed, Supply to action.

(f) Tan, W. said that the enhanced scale was available from stock and would not require procurement. He asked that the requirement be written into the movement order rather than passed verbally.

(g) Okonjo, B. said that the works programme was in one respect helped by the measure, in that works on the corridor north of the checkpoint can now be carried out in the closed period without any interaction with traffic. He asked whether the Group would support night working for the verge drainage scheme on the Marrow Cut approaches. The Chair said this was a matter for the Estate Works Board but that the Group had no objection in principle and would say so.

(h) Petrakis, V. raised lighting at the Node B holding area, which had been adequate for a rolling pattern but is now used by serials assembling before first light. He will bring a costed proposal to the Estate Works Board.

(i) Marchetti, D. said that the consortium had been informed of the measure and had adjusted its own movements. Consortium traffic on AMBER is now daylight only. He reported no friction.

(j) Iyer, S. said that the largest residual risk was schedule fragility. Under the previous pattern, a delay of an hour could be absorbed by running later. Under the daylight corridor there is no later. A delay of an hour against the 1130Z release converts a same-day serial into an overnight lay-up, with the stores, welfare and medical consequences that follow. He asked that the Group accept a hard release cut-off rather than allowing serials to be released "on the margin" in the hope of making the timings. The Chair agreed and directed that a serial not released by 1130Z is to be replanned as a lay-up serial or cancelled, and not released late.

4.6 **Direction.** The Chair summarised. The overnight closure at Checkpoint 2 is a standing constraint on Route AMBER, is in force under MEM-2026-041, and is to be planned around, not planned against. AMBER is a daylight corridor. The Movement Desk is to enter the constraint in the register citing MEM-2026-041 as its authority, with the effective date, and is to ensure it appears on route card AMBER/2026/2, in the detachment standing orders at W-19, and on the face of the daily tasking sheet. No serial is to be planned on the assumption of passing the checkpoint in the closed period.
