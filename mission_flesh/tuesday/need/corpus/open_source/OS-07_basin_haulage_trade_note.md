# Basin Haulage Review

Trade note: what operators are paying on the basin runs
Date: not stated
Byline: staff writer

**Classification:** TRAINING SYNTHETIC - EXERCISE NORTHWIND SHELF. All units, organisations, people, places and events in this document are invented.

---

Rates on general haulage in the Cordell basin have held steadier than most of our readers expected, but the shape of the cost is changing.

Operators we spoke to are quoting between 0.42 and 0.55 credits per tonne-kilometre on basin work, with the spread explained almost entirely by whether the run crosses the Selwyn. The crossing is the variable, and it is a variable in two directions.

Southern work goes over by the vehicle ferry at Ferry Point. The toll is charged by vehicle class and the heavy goods class is not cheap, but the greater cost is waiting. Drivers are paid from the moment they join the queue at the slip, and the queue is unpredictable. Two operators told us they now build an hour of waiting into every southern quote and eat the difference when it runs longer. When the operator does not sail, which happens when the river is up, the load either waits or goes north.

North means the Selwyn Bridge, rated at 40 tonnes and adequate for anything a general haulier is running. The bridge itself is not the problem. The problem is the checkpoint below it, which passes no traffic overnight, so the northern run is a daylight run. That removes the night trunk from the timetable and compresses everything into the working day, which in practice means an extra driver or an extra night's subsistence on anything that used to move after dark.

Fuel is flat. Insurance has moved: two brokers reported a loading on river crossing work at renewal, on the strength of nothing more than the fact of the crossing.

Flat trailer hire is unchanged at 96 credits a week. Curtain-siders are dearer and short.

The advice from the older hands is unchanged and worth repeating: quote the waiting, not the distance.
