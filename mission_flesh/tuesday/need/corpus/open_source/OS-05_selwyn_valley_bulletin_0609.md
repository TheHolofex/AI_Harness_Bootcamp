## Hauliers count the cost of waiting for the gate

By Derek Onslow

The northern road out of the valley has passed no traffic overnight since the spring, and the operators who use it say the queue that builds up waiting for the barrier to lift is taking the best part of their morning.

The restriction is set out in a standing instruction issued by the support group at Station Halberd, numbered MEM-2026-041. Under it, Checkpoint 2 passes no traffic between 2200Z and 0500Z. Movements on the northern corridor, which the group's signage calls the AMBER corridor, are worked in daylight only as a result.

Mr Aled Prosser, of Prosser and Sons at Kilmartin, said the effect on a small operator was worse than the hours suggested. "You cannot run a night trunk any more, so everything is a day job. By the time the barrier lifts there are lorries back to the bend, and if you are seventh or eighth in that queue you have lost an hour before you have moved a metre. Then you are into the morning traffic at Nether Selwyn. We have taken to sending the driver up the evening before and putting him in a bed and breakfast, which is not what anybody would call efficient."

Mrs H. Tregarron, who sits on the parish council, said her concern was the bend rather than the economics. "There are vehicles standing on a blind bend in the dark. Somebody is going to come round that in a hurry one morning."

Mr D. Marchetti, the group's liaison officer, attended the parish council meeting on 2 June. He said the overnight closure at Checkpoint 2 was in force and that the group was working the northern corridor as a daylight corridor accordingly. He said advance warning signs had been put out on both approaches after a report of a tailback in May, that a marshal was now on the approach at reopening, and that drivers were asked to hold back at the lay-by rather than close up on the bend. He said he was not able to discuss the group's own movements.

The parish council resolved to write asking for a longer signed approach and for the lay-by to be swept, it having filled with grit over the winter.

## Verge fire near the Marrow Cut

By Marjorie Twiss

A verge fire on the northern road below the Marrow Cut took two appliances and the best part of three hours on the afternoon of Thursday 28 May. No property was involved and nobody was hurt. The fire service believes it started at the roadside and asks people not to throw cigarettes from vehicles, a request it makes every dry spell and which every dry spell ignores.

The verge is blackened for perhaps two hundred metres below the cattle grid. The county says it will not reseed until the autumn.

## Hall roof fund passes halfway

By Marjorie Twiss

The Kilmartin hall roof fund has passed 11,000 credits following the fete and a donation from the Cordell Basin Relief Consortium's community fund. The committee has asked three roofers for prices and expects to choose in July. Mr Cadogan said the aim is still to have the work done before the winter, and that if the fund falls short the committee will borrow the remainder against next year's fete.

## Ferry Point application draws eleven letters

By staff

Cordell Basin District Council has received eleven written representations on the application by Selwyn Ferry Services Ltd for hardstanding, a waiting area, lighting and drainage beside the Ferry Point approach road, reference CB/2026/0147. Nine object, two support.

The objections are almost all about the six lighting columns and the effect on the houses at the top of the lane. Two raise the loss of the rough grazing. One raises the drainage. The parish council has resolved not to object but to ask for the columns to be lowered and fitted with cowls.

The council's development control committee is expected to take the application in July.
