## Ferry running well as summer traffic peaks

The Ferry Point crossing has had its busiest month since the spring, with
operator Selwyn Ferry Services reporting steady demand from relief traffic and
local users alike.

"July is always our month," said operations manager M. Dunphy. "The river is low,
the days are long, and everyone who has been putting off a crossing does it now."

The operator confirmed that MV Tolland Carrier has worked every scheduled day
since the beginning of June. Asked about the autumn, Mr Dunphy was more cautious.
"The river decides in September. It always has. We work when we can work."

## Bridge works still awaited

At the northern crossing, the Selwyn Bridge remains without the structural works
that were first discussed more than a year ago.

A spokesperson for the Basin Structures Cell said the works were specified and
that scheduling was "a matter for the contractor". The contractor, Ardwick
Structural, did not respond to a request for comment before this bulletin went to
press.

Local hauliers have complained that the uncertainty makes planning difficult. "We
are told the bridge is fine and we are told the works are urgent," said one
operator who asked not to be named. "It cannot be both."

## Road surface at the Marrow Cut

Drainage work at the Marrow Cut defile has improved the standing water problem
but not solved it, according to users. The Basin Structures Cell survey of April
confirmed the clearance through the defile at 4.1 metres, ending a period of
confusion in which two different figures were in circulation.

## In brief

The Node D fuel point will close for maintenance on 3 August for one day. The
Cordell Basin Relief Consortium has extended its hardstanding hire agreement at
Node D to the end of the year.
