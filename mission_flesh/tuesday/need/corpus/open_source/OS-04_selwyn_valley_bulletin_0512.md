## Kilmartin school fete beats last year's total

By Marjorie Twiss

Kilmartin Primary took over the playing field on Saturday for the spring fete and came away with 1,240 credits for the hall roof fund, a little over last year's figure and the best return since the fete moved from the churchyard.

The tombola sold out before noon. Mrs Enid Prowse, who has run the cake stall for nineteen years and says every year that it will be her last, reported that the lemon drizzle went first, as it always does. The fire service brought an appliance and let the smaller children sit in the cab, which the head teacher, Mr Cadogan, described afterwards as the single most effective fundraising decision the committee has ever made.

The committee thanks Selwyn Ferry Services for the loan of the marquee, the Kilmartin bowls club for the trestles, and the four sixth formers who took the whole thing down in the rain on Sunday morning without being asked twice.

The hall roof fund now stands at just under 9,000 credits. The committee's estimate for the work is nearer 21,000. Donations may be left at the post office.

## B2107 to close for eleven days at Tollerton Cross

By Derek Onslow

The county surfacing contractor will close the B2107 through Tollerton Cross to through traffic from Monday 18 May, with the road due to reopen on Thursday 28 May. The work is planing and relaying between the war memorial and the top of Slaughden Hill, where the surface has been breaking up since the winter.

A signed diversion will run by way of Nether Selwyn and the C412. Access will be kept for residents, for the milk collection and for the school bus, which will turn at the memorial rather than at the top of the hill. Night working has not been permitted, so drivers should expect the closure to be in place through the daytime only, with the road open to a single lane under lights overnight.

The council's highways office asks that vehicles are not left on the verges at the diversion pinch point below Nether Selwyn, where two lorries met last autumn and neither could pass.

## Ferry sailings normal through April

By Derek Onslow

Selwyn Ferry Services report that the vehicle ferry at Ferry Point ran to timetable throughout April with no cancelled sailings.

Mr M. Dunphy, ferry operations manager, said the MV Tolland Carrier had come through her spring maintenance without difficulty and had gone back on service on schedule. Asked about interruptions, he said the only ones worth mentioning were the usual ones. "We do not sail when the river is up," he said. "The master calls it, and when he calls it we stop. The rest of the year we sail, and we sail to the timetable."

The ticket office at the top of the slip is now open from first sailing to last, having kept shorter hours over the winter. Foot passengers continue to travel free on the mid-morning sailings on Wednesdays, an arrangement the company says will stay in place at least until the autumn.

## Planning: waiting area proposed at Ferry Point

By staff

An application has been lodged with Cordell Basin District Council for hardstanding, a vehicle waiting area, lighting and drainage on land next to the Ferry Point approach road. The applicant is Selwyn Ferry Services Ltd.

The application reference is CB/2026/0147. Plans may be seen at the council offices at Corbery during office hours, and representations must be in writing by the middle of June.

Nether Selwyn parish council will take the application at its June meeting. The parish clerk said she had already had three telephone calls about the lighting columns and none at all about the drainage, which she thought was the wrong way round.
