## Summer traffic tests the valley roads

By Derek Onslow

The valley has had its busiest fortnight of the year and the roads have shown it.

The campsite at Tollerton Cross has been full since the end of July. The overflow field is open. The consequence, as every summer, is that the lane below the campsite has caravans on both verges by the middle of the afternoon and nothing larger than a car can get through.

Two farmers at Tollerton have written to the council about blocked gateways. Mr J. Ardagh said he had been unable to get a trailer out of the top field on three days running and had eventually taken the long way round through Kilmartin, which he described as a forty minute answer to a two minute problem.

The police have asked drivers to use the marked parking at the village hall, where there is room, and have said they will move vehicles obstructing gateways. The council has repainted the passing places on the lane and says it has no money for more this year.

Traffic at the ferry has been heavy on Saturdays. The company asks drivers to join the queue from the top of the lane and not from the field gate, which is a private access.

## Diesel smell at Bastion Reach: two accounts

By Marjorie Twiss

Readers at Bastion Reach reported a strong smell of diesel on the evening of Monday 15 June, followed by absorbent booms laid in the roadside ditch and a contractor's vehicle on the hardstanding for most of the following day.

The version that has gone round the village since is that one of the group's own tankers lost its load. That is not what the group says. A spokesman for the safety cell at Station Halberd told the Bulletin that an investigation had been completed, that the vehicle involved was not a group asset, and that it belonged to a civil partner working on the site.

The Cordell Basin Relief Consortium confirmed that one of its vehicles was involved and that a delivery hose failed during a transfer. A spokesperson said the spill was caught on hardstanding and in the site drainage, that nothing reached the river, and that the hose and its fittings had since been replaced across the consortium's tanker fleet.

The Bulletin puts both accounts on the record. Nobody was hurt. The ditch has been cleared and the booms lifted.

## Letters to the Editor

Letters should carry a name and an address, though we will withhold the address on request.

**Verges**

Sir, the county cut the verges on the C412 in the last week of June and has not been back since. The growth on the bend below Nether Selwyn is now taller than a car and you cannot see a thing coming out of the junction. I have telephoned four times and been told each time that it is on the schedule. Whose schedule, and for when?

M. Rowntree, Nether Selwyn

**The raffle**

Sir, I am sorry to raise it again but the third prize in the fete raffle was drawn on ticket 214 and announced as 241, and the hamper went to the wrong house. I have said nothing for three months. I say it now only because the committee has printed the same numbers in its accounts. I do not want the hamper. I would like the accounts corrected.

Name and address supplied

**Convoys before dawn**

Sir, we have had lorries forming up on the lay-by at the top of the north road from half past three in the morning, several days a week, since the spring. Engines running, doors, men talking, and then the whole lot pulls away together at about five.

I understand that the road they use, what the signs call the AMBER corridor, is worked in the daytime, and I understand why the vehicles have to be ready before the gate opens. What I do not understand is why they have to be ready on a lay-by two hundred metres from four houses, when there is a perfectly good hardstanding a mile further up with nobody living near it.

I have written to the liaison officer and had a courteous reply saying the matter would be looked at. That was in June.

T. Alderney, Kilmartin

**Thank you**

Sir, may I thank the twenty-two people who turned out for the defibrillator training at the village hall, and the nurse who came out on her own evening to give it. We have the machine on the wall. Now we have people who know what to do with it.

E. Prowse, Nether Selwyn
