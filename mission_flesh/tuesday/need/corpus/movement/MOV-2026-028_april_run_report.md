## 1. Summary

Twenty-six serials programmed, twenty-three run, one cancelled, two deferred.
Both corridors open throughout. Programme discipline on AMBER improved on the
March figure, largely because the desk stopped accepting late demands after
1600Z the preceding day.

## 2. Working of Route AMBER

Checkpoint 2 (W-19) passes no traffic between 2200Z and 0500Z. The closure is in
force under MEM-2026-041 and is treated as a standing condition of the corridor.
AMBER is therefore worked as a daylight corridor and serials are timed against
the gate, not against the destination:

a. Earliest SP from Station Halberd is set so that the head of the serial
   arrives at the Checkpoint 2 southern holding area no earlier than 0450Z. In
   practice this puts SP between 0535Z and 0630Z depending on load and lane
   arrangements at the surfacing works.

b. Last northbound clearance of Checkpoint 2 is planned for 2030Z, giving ninety
   minutes of margin against the closure. The desk will not release a serial
   from Station Halberd that cannot make that clearance on planning times.

c. Southbound returns that cannot clear by 2030Z lay over at W-41 or W-19 and
   run on the following day's first slot. Four serials laid over in April, all
   at W-41, all planned rather than reactive.

d. Any task that cannot be completed inside the daylight window is passed to
   CINDER at the demand stage rather than attempted on AMBER and cut short.

a. Layover at W-41 is now routine rather than exceptional and the detachment

b. The 1600Z demand cut-off has held. Three late demands were refused in April

c. Escort tasking was heavy in the second week and one Consortium request was
   met at short notice by re-roling a return crew. Policy on escort allocation
