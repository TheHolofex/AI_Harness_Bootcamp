## 4. Commentary

a. Fleet availability at the end of June stands at 84 per cent, against a
   working target of 85 per cent. This is the closest the fleet has come to the
   target since February and reflects the workshop clearing its own backlog
   rather than any reduction in demand.

b. The binding shortage remains escort vehicles. Six serviceable against a daily
   requirement that peaked at five in the third week of June leaves no depth for
   a breakdown. One escort vehicle has been off road for most of the month on
   accident repair and a second is awaiting a single panel. Supply is asked to
   press the panel order.

c. Tanker availability is adequate for the current programme but a second fuel
   tanker failure would put bulk POL on both corridors at risk in the same week.
   There is no hire arrangement in place for tankers and the desk recommends one
   be explored.

d. Rough terrain forklift availability at four of six continues to slow offload
   at the forward detachments and is the main reason for the long completion
   times seen on works lifts. The obsolete mast roller on MER/2210 should be
   treated as a write-off decision rather than a repair.

e. No vehicle was lost to a defect discovered on the road during June. Pre-move
   checks caught two faults at Station Halberd before SP, which is the intent.
