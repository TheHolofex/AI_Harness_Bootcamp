## 1. Scope

This policy governs the allocation of escort vehicles and escort crews to
movement serials on Routes AMBER and CINDER. It applies to all Task Group
MERIDIAN moves and to accompanied moves run on behalf of the Cordell Basin
Relief Consortium.

## 2. Authority to task

a. Escorts are tasked by the Movement Desk and by no one else. Detachment
   commanders may request; they may not task.

b. Outside desk hours the Duty Officer may task an escort for a move that cannot
   wait, and informs the desk at the next opening.

c. A serial commander who finds himself without a tasked escort at SP does not
   proceed on his own judgement. He reports to the desk and the serial is held
   or re-programmed.

d. The Consortium requests escorts through Liaison. Requests are met on the same
   basis as internal demands and are not given priority by virtue of being
   external.

## 3. Escort scale

| Serial type | Escort scale | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine load, up to four vehicles | Nil | Serial commander in lead vehicle |
| Routine load, five vehicles or more | One, rear | Rear escort maintains the tail and manages overtaking traffic |
| Any serial carrying POL in bulk | One, rear | Regardless of serial size |
| Abnormal load (width, length or overhang beyond standard) | Two, front and rear | Front escort clears junctions and pinch points |
| Plant and low loader moves | Two, front and rear | Banksman carried in the front escort |
| Any move through the Marrow Cut defile with more than four vehicles | One, front | Defile working, see para 5 |
| Any move over the Selwyn Bridge with more than four vehicles | One, front | Span working, see para 5 |
| Accompanied Consortium move | As for the equivalent internal serial | No uplift for external status |
| Single vehicle, routine task | Nil | |
| VIP or visitor movement | One, front | Tasked by name against the visit programme |

## 4. Priority when demand exceeds supply

Where escorts cannot be found for every serial on the programme, they are
allocated in this order:

1. Moves that are legally or physically incapable of proceeding without an
   escort, in the order in which they were demanded.
2. Medical and aid station resupply.
3. Moves that would otherwise fall outside the Checkpoint 2 daylight window and
   be lost for the day.
4. Bulk POL.
5. Routine sustainment.
6. Backloads, empties and waste.

A serial that cannot be escorted is deferred, not run unescorted. There is no
authority below SO2 Operations to run an escorted category unescorted, and that
authority has not been delegated to the desk.

## 5. Conduct of the escort

a. **Front escort** travels not more than 400 m ahead of the head of the serial,
   in radio contact throughout, and clears junctions, works entrances and pinch
   points before the serial arrives. At the Marrow Cut defile and at the Selwyn
   Bridge the front escort confirms the way is clear and controls entry to the
   restricted stretch.

b. **Rear escort** travels behind the last vehicle, keeps the tail closed up,
   and reports any vehicle that drops out. The rear escort does not overtake the
   serial except to recover a broken-down vehicle.

c. **Radio.** Escorts work on the serial net. An escort that loses communication
   with the serial commander halts the serial at the next safe point.

d. **Escorts do not carry load.** An escort vehicle is not a spare cargo
   vehicle and will not be loaded to make up capacity.

## 6. Crewing and hours

a. Escort crews are two up. Single crewing is not authorised.

b. Escort crews count against the same duty day limits as serial crews. An
   escort crew that has run out to W-31 and back does not then take a second
   task the same day.

c. Escort drivers must hold the qualification for the route being escorted. The
   matrix at MOV-2026-079 is the authority.

## 7. Returns

The desk records escort hours by serial and returns them monthly with the run
report. Detachments provide escort hours worked on local tasks by the fifth of
the following month.
