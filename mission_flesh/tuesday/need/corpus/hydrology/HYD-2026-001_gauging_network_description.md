## 1. Scope

The basin network comprises five telemetered river stations, three on the Selwyn
and two on the Cordell system. All five report to the basin gauge series. This
document describes what each station is, where it sits, and what its record does
and does not represent. Values are not reproduced here. Every figure quoted from
this network must be taken from the basin gauge series, against the station and
timestamp it belongs to.

## 3. Station notes

**SEL-01, Upper Selwyn at Marrow.** Bedrock and boulder control, section confined
between rock walls for most of its range. The station responds first in the
network and falls first. The control is stable in ordinary conditions but is
known to shift after large events when boulders move in the throat; the rating is
checked by gauging after any event that reworks the bed. Access is by the mill
track, which is gated and can be soft after rain. The site has no floodplain: the
water either sits in the channel or it does not, and the record reflects that.

**SEL-04, Selwyn at Ferry Point reach.** Sand and gravel bed, mobile section,
sited on the gauging pier to keep the sensor clear of the ferry slipway apron.
This is the most heavily used record in the network because of the crossing, and
it is also the least stable section: the bed scours and fills through the year,
and the rating is revised more often here than anywhere else in the basin. Levels
at this station are affected by the geometry of the reach in front of the pier,
which is not the same geometry as the slipway itself. The station reports water
level in the reach. It does not report conditions on the slipway, on the apron,
or at the ferry berth, and it must not be read as though it did. Operating
decisions for the crossing are the operator's, made against the operator's own
criteria; the Hydrometric Service publishes the level and nothing else.

**SEL-07, Selwyn at Bastion Reach.** Wide, flat, slow reach with an extensive
floodplain on the right bank. The station is backwater influenced whenever the
Cordell is high, which means the relationship between level and flow at this site
is not single valued: the same level can occur on a rising limb and a falling
limb with materially different discharge. Level is reported; flow at this station
is derived with a loop rating and carries a wider uncertainty than elsewhere.
Users interested in flow rather than stage should read the note on the loop
rating in the station metadata before using the derived series.

**COR-02, Cordell at Node D.** Bridge section, artificial control, generally the
best behaved station in the network. The section is surveyed annually and the
rating has needed only minor adjustment since installation. The intake is prone
to silting after high flows and is cleared on the routine visit. Debris racking on
the upstream bridge pier has on two occasions raised the recorded level above the
true reach level; both episodes were identified and the affected periods flagged.

**COR-05, Cordell tributary at Halberd.** Flume, small catchment, very flashy.
This station rises and falls faster than anything else in the network and is
frequently at the bottom of its range in dry weather. It is useful as an early
indicator that rain has reached the estate ground. It is not representative of
the Cordell main stem and never has been. Its record is retained mainly for the
estate drainage programme.

## 4. Reading across the network

The five stations sit at different points in the catchment, on different
controls, in channels of different shape, and against separately levelled datums.
They read differently from one another, by design and by physics. Three
consequences follow, and all three are frequently got wrong by users in a hurry.

First, **a level quoted for one reach does not transfer to another.** There is no
constant offset between stations and there never will be, because the offset
between any two stations varies with the size of the event, the direction of the
storm, which sub-catchment is contributing, and whether the river is rising or
falling. A figure from SEL-01 is a statement about the channel at Marrow. It is
not a statement about Ferry Point, about Bastion Reach, or about the Cordell.

Second, **a level is meaningless without its station.** A number carried from one
document to another without its identifier has lost the only thing that made it
interpretable. Quote the station with the figure, always, and quote the timestamp
with both.

Third, **the stations peak at different times.** The upper stations peak before
the lower ones during a rain-driven event, and the interval between them is not
fixed. A reader who takes a peak at one station as the peak for the basin will be
early at the bottom of the catchment and late at the top. See HYD-2026-009.

## 5. Data

All figures from this network are published in the basin gauge series and only
there. This document is a description of the network, not a source of values.
Requests for historic data, for gauging records, or for rating curve history go
