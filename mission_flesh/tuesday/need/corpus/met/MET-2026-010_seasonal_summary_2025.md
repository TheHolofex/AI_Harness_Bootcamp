# Estate Surface Sensor Network: Seasonal Summary 2025

**Issued by:** Estate Works Section, Instrumentation Cell, Task Group MERIDIAN, Station Halberd
**Reference:** MET-2026-010
**Date of issue:** 27 January 2026
**Season covered:** 01 May to 30 September 2025
**Prepared by:** Okafor, B., Instrumentation Cell
**Approved by:** Estate Works Section head
**Distribution:** Estate Works file; Movement Desk; users of the estate surface sensor export

**Classification:** TRAINING SYNTHETIC - EXERCISE NORTHWIND SHELF. All units, organisations, people, places and events in this document are invented.

## 1. General character of the season

The 2025 season was, in the round, settled and unexceptional. It began cool and
unsettled, turned dry and clear through the middle part, and closed cloudier and
wetter. Nothing occurred that fell outside the range the network has recorded in
previous seasons, and no sensor recorded a condition that required investigation
on grounds other than instrument health.

The season is described here in qualitative terms only. Every figure is held in
the estate surface sensor export, against the sensor identifier and timestamp it
belongs to, and none is reproduced.

## 2. Progress of the season

**May** was cool, cloudy and windy, with frequent rain. These are the conditions
in which the network agrees with itself most closely: cloud removes the radiative
differences between exposures, wind couples every surface to the air above it,
and wetting removes what is left. For much of the month the eight sensors tracked
one another closely enough that the differences between them were of little
practical interest. This is the least informative kind of weather for a surface
network and the easiest to read.

**June** brought a change to clearer, lighter-wind conditions in the second half
of the month. The spread across the network widened accordingly, and the daily
pattern that dominates the rest of this summary established itself: wide
divergence between sensors through the day, convergence overnight, and a further
divergence at night in the opposite direction between sensors with an open sky
view and those without.

**July** was the driest and clearest part of the season, with a long settled spell
in the middle of the month under light winds. The spread across the network was
at its widest in this period. Diurnal ranges at the open, unshaded positions were
correspondingly large, and the shaded position in the Marrow Cut behaved almost
as a different climate for the duration.

**August** was mixed, with the settled pattern broken by two unsettled spells that
brought cloud, wind and rain. The network converged during those spells and
diverged again between them. The last week of the month was clear and light-wind
again.

**September** turned progressively cloudier and wetter, with the first sustained
autumnal conditions arriving in the second half of the month. Diurnal ranges
narrowed across the network. Nights became longer and the night-time behaviour of
the open positions became the more prominent feature of the record, with clear
calm nights producing the widest divergence in that direction of the whole season.

## 3. Behaviour of individual positions

**Selwyn Bridge, three positions.** The three bridge sensors diverged from one
another throughout the season, exactly as the siting record leads one to expect.
The mid-span position (SLW-DECK-02) moved fastest and furthest in both
directions, being over air and water, unshaded, and exposed on both faces of the
deck. The south approach (SLW-DECK-01) lagged, held back by the mass of the
abutment fill and sheltered from the wind. The north approach (SLW-DECK-03) was
consistently the last to respond in the morning owing to shading by the cutting
and the tree line. The three converged in cloud, wind and rain and diverged in
clear, still conditions, and there was no part of the season in which a stable
relationship between them could have been established. Anyone who had fitted an
offset between the approaches and the mid-span in July would have found it
useless by the end of September.

**CP2-PAD-01, Checkpoint 2.** The concrete pad behaved as a high thermal mass
surface throughout: slow to change, narrow diurnal range relative to the bitumen
positions, and lagging behind conditions. The record for this sensor is
interrupted irregularly by the shading effect of standing vehicles in bay 3,
which is not a fault and is not corrected for. Periods in which a vehicle stood
over or beside the sensor are visible in the record as abrupt departures from the
pattern of the surrounding hours.

**MRW-ROAD-01, Marrow Cut.** The outlier of the network in every settled spell.
Deep shade from the defile walls for most of the day, near total wind shelter,
and persistent surface seepage combined to produce a record quite unlike anything
else the network holds. This sensor is the clearest demonstration available that
a surface figure is a property of one place.

**FPT-SLIP-01, Ferry Point slipway.** Alternated between dry and wetted regimes
through the season according to ramp operations and river conditions. In the
wetted regime the record is dominated by evaporation and departs from the other
open positions entirely. This sensor is the least useful in the network for any
purpose other than describing the slipway apron itself.

**NDD-PAD-01, Node D.** The fastest responding surface in the network, being dark
bitumen over a thin base. Largest diurnal ranges of the eight in clear conditions.

**BRH-PAD-01, Bastion Reach.** Followed the other open concrete position at
Checkpoint 2 in general character, with an afternoon departure caused by shading
from the stores building and a persistent moderating influence from the river
breeze off the lower Selwyn.

## 4. Data availability

Availability was good across the season. The principal losses were a logger fault
at Node D in early July and a duct flooding incident at Bastion Reach in late
August, both recorded in the export gap schedule at MET-2026-013. The Checkpoint 2
unit was replaced before the season began following the April calibration
failure, and the replacement performed without incident.

## 5. Standing caution

A summary of a past season describes that season. It is not a description of any
other season, and it should not be used as an expectation for one. The features
described above followed from the particular sequence of weather that occurred:
change the cloud, the wind or the rainfall and the same eight sensors in the same
eight positions will produce a different record.

Where a figure is needed for any date, in this season or any other, take it from
the estate surface sensor export.
