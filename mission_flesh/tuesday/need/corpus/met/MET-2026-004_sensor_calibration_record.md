## 1. Calibration policy

Every sensor in the network is calibrated on a twelve-month cycle, and out of
cycle after any of the following: replacement of the unit, disturbance of the
mounting, works to the surrounding surfacing, a suspected fault raised by the
automatic checks, or a manual comparison that fails at a site visit.

Two methods are in use.

**Bench comparison (method B).** The sensor is removed from its mounting and
compared against a traceable reference probe in a stirred bath at two set points
spanning the working range, with a dwell at each point until both instruments are
stable. This is the reference method. It requires the sensor to be taken out of
service, the surfacing to be broken out and reinstated, and the site to be closed
to traffic for the visit, so it is used at scheduled calibration and after
faults.

**In situ comparison (method I).** A calibrated portable probe is placed in
contact with the surface adjacent to the installed sensor and the two are
compared over a settling period, in stable overcast conditions and out of direct
sun. This method carries a wider uncertainty than the bench method and is used
for interim checks and for confirming that a sensor is still within tolerance
between scheduled calibrations. A failure at in situ comparison triggers a bench
calibration; a pass does not substitute for one.

Tolerance is the manufacturer's stated tolerance for the sensor type, as recorded
in the instrument file. Findings below are expressed against that tolerance.
Measured values, offsets and reference readings are held in the calibration
certificates lodged with the Instrumentation Cell and are not reproduced here.

## 3. Access notes affecting the programme

**Checkpoint 2 (CP2-PAD-01).** Checkpoint 2 passes no traffic between 2200Z and
0500Z under MEM-2026-041, which is currently in force. Calibration visits are
therefore scheduled in daylight and coordinated with the holding pad bay
allocation so that bay 3 is kept clear for the duration of the visit. The
April 2026 visit ran late because bay 3 was occupied on arrival.

**Selwyn Bridge (SLW-DECK-01, -02, -03).** Bench calibration requires a lane
closure and breaking out of the surfacing at the sensor pocket. Closures are
booked through the Movement Desk with not less than ten working days' notice. The
three sensors are calibrated on consecutive days where possible to hold the
number of closures down, and the March 2026 round achieved this.

**Marrow Cut (MRW-ROAD-01).** Single carriageway at the narrows with no passing
place; the visit requires traffic control at both ends of the defile. Persistent
seepage across the surface has twice delayed reinstatement of the sensor pocket
while the surfacing cured.

**Ferry Point (FPT-SLIP-01).** Visits are coordinated with Selwyn Ferry Services
Ltd through Dunphy, M., and are taken between sailings. The apron environment is
hard on the installation; this sensor has needed adjustment at every scheduled
calibration since installation and its sensing face is now pitted.

## 4. Standing notes

A calibration establishes that a sensor measures its own surface correctly. It
establishes nothing whatever about whether that surface is representative of any
other surface. A well calibrated sensor at an approach and a well calibrated
sensor at mid-span will disagree, and both will be right. See MET-2026-002.

Where a sensor has been adjusted, the export is not retrospectively corrected for
the period before adjustment unless the drift was found to be outside tolerance,
in which case the affected period is reflagged and the reflagging is recorded in
the export change log. Users holding extracts taken before a reflagging hold
superseded data.
