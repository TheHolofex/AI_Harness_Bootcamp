## 1. Scope

The estate surface sensor network comprises eight surface temperature sensors
installed across estate assets at Station Halberd and its detachments. All eight
report to the estate surface sensor export. This document records what each
sensor is, where it sits and what it is exposed to. No readings are reproduced
here; every figure from this network is held in the export against the sensor
identifier and timestamp it belongs to.

## 3. Installation

All eight sensors are flush-mounted surface probes set into the wearing course or
slab, with the sensing face in the plane of the trafficked surface. Cabling runs
in ducts to a local logger at each site, and the loggers report to the estate
collector. The three Selwyn Bridge sensors were installed by Ardwick Structural
under the deck surfacing works and share a single duct run and logger at the
north abutment; the remaining five were installed by Estate Works and each has
its own logger.

The bridge installations differ from the others in one respect that matters for
interpretation. The two approach sensors sit in surfacing over abutment fill,
which is to say over ground. The mid-span sensor sits in surfacing over a
structural deck with air beneath it. These are different constructions with
different thermal behaviour, and the difference is not a defect in the
installation. It is the reason all three sensors exist.

## 4. Readings differ between sensors on the same asset

This section is stated plainly because it is the point most often lost when
figures leave the export.

**The three Selwyn Bridge sensors read differently from one another, routinely
and markedly.** They are on the same structure, within a few hundred paces of one
another, and they do not agree. SLW-DECK-02 at mid-span sits over air and over
water, is unshaded, and is exposed to wind on both faces of the deck; it gains
and loses heat faster than either approach and follows conditions closely.
SLW-DECK-01 at the south approach sits over solid fill with the thermal inertia
of the embankment behind it, is sheltered from the wind, and lags. SLW-DECK-03 at
the north approach is shaded for the first part of the day by the cutting and the
tree line and behaves differently again. The three diverge most in clear, still,
sunny conditions and converge in overcast, windy or wet ones. There is no fixed
offset between them and no reliable way to derive one from another.

**A reading from an approach sensor is not a reading for the mid-span.** SLW-DECK-01
and SLW-DECK-03 describe the surfacing over the abutments. SLW-DECK-02 describes
the surfacing over the main span. Where the mid-span condition is what matters,
read SLW-DECK-02. Substituting an approach value because it is more convenient,
more recent, or the only one to hand is not a conservative approximation; it is a
measurement of a different piece of the structure.

The same applies across assets. The concrete pad at Checkpoint 2, the bitumen pad
at Node D, the shaded carriageway in the Marrow Cut and the wetted apron at Ferry
Point are four different materials in four different exposures. They will not
agree with each other and they are not intended to. Each sensor is a measurement
of its own square of ground and of nothing else.

## 5. Quoting a reading

A value from this network is interpretable only with its sensor identifier and
its timestamp. Both travel with the value or the value should not be used. A
figure that has reached a document, a signal or a decision log without them
cannot be recovered, because there is no way to establish which surface it
described.
