# Repair Specification - North Slip Lower Apron, Ferry Point (W-22)

**Reference:** ENG-2026-012
**Revision:** A, for tender
**Issued:** 9 March 2026
**Prepared by:** Estate Works, Station Halberd
**Technical review:** Jankowski, D., Ardwick Structural
**Asset:** Ferry Point north slip, register W-22/MAR/01
**Distribution:** Estate Works file; W-22; Liaison (Marchetti, D.); Selwyn Ferry Services Ltd for information (Dunphy, M.)

**Classification:** TRAINING SYNTHETIC - EXERCISE NORTHWIND SHELF. All units, organisations, people, places and events in this document are invented.

## 1. General

1.1 This specification covers the repair of spalled reinforced concrete to the lower apron of the north slip at Ferry Point. It does not cover the upper apron, the slip walls, the tie downs or the ramp bearing area, all of which were reported serviceable at ENG-2025-027.

1.2 The north slip is not in daily use. MV Tolland Carrier works the south slip and will continue to do so throughout. The north slip is to be handed to the contractor for the duration of the works and returned clean and clear.

1.3 All work is to be carried out by operatives experienced in concrete repair in a river environment. The contractor is to nominate a supervisor who will be present whenever work is in progress on the apron.

## 2. Extent of defect

2.1 Spalling affects the lower apron over an area recorded in September 2025 as approximately 11 square metres in four separate patches, the largest about 4.5 square metres. Depth of loss is generally 30 to 60 mm with local pockets to 90 mm.

2.2 Reinforcement is exposed in three of the four patches. Where exposed it carries loose brown corrosion product with pitting; measured loss of bar diameter at the worst point was a little over 10 per cent. No bar is severed.

2.3 The concrete around the patches sounds hollow on hammer tapping over a further margin of 100 to 250 mm in most places. The contractor is to expect the repair area to grow on breakout and is to price accordingly; a provisional quantity of 16 square metres is to be carried.

## 3. Working conditions

3.1 The lower apron is submerged for part of each day. Work on it can only be prosecuted during the exposed period, which is short, varies day to day, and is further reduced when the river is up. Selwyn Ferry Services Ltd suspends operations when the river is up and the contractor should expect the same conditions to stop apron work.

3.2 The contractor is to agree a daily working window with the site supervisor each morning based on observed conditions. No work is to be planned on the assumption of a fixed window. Every operation on the apron is to be capable of being made safe and abandoned at short notice; no breakout is to be opened that cannot be covered before the water returns.

3.3 The contractor is to allow in the programme for lost days. A repair of this extent would take a fortnight in a dry environment; the realistic allowance here is six to eight weeks.

3.4 A watchman is to be posted with a clear view upstream whenever operatives are on the apron. Life jackets, a throw line and a manned recovery craft are required at all times when the apron is occupied.

## 4. Breakout

4.1 Delaminated and hollow sounding concrete is to be removed by hand held breakers of not more than 10 kg, or by hydrodemolition where the contractor prefers it and can demonstrate control of the debris.

4.2 Breakout is to continue until sound concrete is reached in all directions. Where reinforcement is exposed, breakout is to continue behind the bar to give a clear 25 mm all round, so that the repair material can be worked fully around the steel.

4.3 Repair perimeters are to be saw cut square to a depth of 15 mm. Feather edges are not permitted. Perimeters are to be squared off in plan; irregular outlines are not acceptable.

4.4 All debris is to be collected and removed from the apron before the water returns. Nothing is to be swept into the river.

## 5. Reinforcement treatment

5.1 Exposed reinforcement is to be cleaned to bright metal by grit blasting or needle gunning, to the standard of a second quality surface, over the full exposed length plus 50 mm beyond the repair perimeter.

5.2 Where measured section loss exceeds 15 per cent the bar is to be supplemented by a new bar of matching diameter lapped in accordance with the design, fixed with proprietary anchors into sound concrete. The contractor is to notify the supervisor before cutting or supplementing any bar.

5.3 Cleaned reinforcement is to receive two coats of cementitious anti-corrosion primer, applied within four hours of cleaning. If the steel is allowed to bloom it is to be recleaned.

5.4 Discrete sacrificial anodes are to be installed at not more than 500 mm centres around the perimeter of each repair patch and tied directly to cleaned reinforcement, to limit incipient anode formation in the parent concrete adjacent to the repairs. Continuity between anode ties and the reinforcement cage is to be proven by resistance measurement and recorded.

## 6. Repair material and placing

6.1 The repair material is to be a proprietary cementitious repair mortar of a grade suited to marine and tidal exposure, with a stated compressive strength not less than that of the parent concrete and low shrinkage. The material is to be certified for placing on damp substrates.

6.2 The substrate is to be saturated surface dry at the moment of placing. Standing water in the breakout is to be removed by sponge or vacuum.

6.3 Material is to be applied by hand in layers of not more than 40 mm, each layer scratched to receive the next. Depths beyond 90 mm are to be built up in successive visits.

6.4 The finished surface is to be struck flush with the surrounding apron and given a wood float finish, not a steel trowel finish. The apron is a trafficked ramp and must retain texture.

## 7. Curing and reinstatement

7.1 Every repair is to be cured under a proprietary curing membrane and covered with a fixed sheet before the water returns, and the covering is to remain until the material has reached the manufacturer's stated early strength.

7.2 Any repair that is submerged before the material has reached early strength is to be presumed defective. The contractor is to cut it out and replace it at his own cost.

7.3 On completion the whole apron is to be swept, the repairs hammer tested and any hollow areas made good, and a record drawing produced showing the final extent of each repair, bar supplements and anode positions.

## 8. Records

The contractor is to keep a daily record of the working window actually achieved, conditions, work done and materials placed, and is to hand a copy to the supervisor at the end of each working day. This record forms part of the completion documentation.

End of specification.
