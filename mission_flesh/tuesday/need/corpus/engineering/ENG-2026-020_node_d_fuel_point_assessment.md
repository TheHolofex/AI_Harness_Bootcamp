# Condition and Capacity Assessment - Fuel Point, Node D (W-07)

**Reference:** ENG-2026-020
**Assessment date:** 28 April 2026
**Assessed by:** Kettleby, J. (Estate Works) with Sgt Ruane, B. (POL, W-07)
**Asset:** Node D bulk fuel point, register W-07/POL/01
**Previous assessment:** ENG-2025-021, August 2025
**Distribution:** Estate Works file; W-07; Supply (Tan, W.); Safety adviser

**Classification:** TRAINING SYNTHETIC - EXERCISE NORTHWIND SHELF. All units, organisations, people, places and events in this document are invented.

## 1. Installation

The fuel point sits on made ground at the eastern edge of the node and consists of a bladder farm, a pump and filtration set, two dispensing points and a small stores and office cabin. It was installed as a temporary facility and has now been in continuous use for over two years, which is the underlying reason for most of what follows.

The bladder farm comprises six collapsible fabric bladders of 20,000 litres nominal each, giving a farm capacity of 120,000 litres, laid out in two rows of three within a common lined bund. Working stock at the time of assessment was a little over 71,000 litres.

## 2. Bladders

All six bladders were inspected externally, and where the layout allowed, underneath by mirror. Four are in good order. Bladder 3 has a scuffed patch about 200 mm across on the upper surface where a hose coupling has been dragged across it repeatedly; the outer coating is worn but the reinforcement is not exposed. Bladder 5 has a weeping seep at the outlet flange collar which was leaving a stain of about 150 mm on the liner beneath; it was retorqued during the visit and was dry on recheck two hours later. It should be watched.

The ground sheeting beneath the bladders is torn in three places and has ridden up at the north end, so that bladder 1 is partly resting on the bund liner rather than on its protective sheet. Bladder 1 should be transferred and the sheeting relaid.

Pressure relief and content gauging are functional on all six. The gauge on bladder 4 reads about 5 per cent high against dipped contents and should be recalibrated or replaced.

## 3. Pump, filtration and hoses

A single diesel driven pump set with a duplex filter water separator serves both dispensing points. The set is in good order, well maintained and clean. Filter differential pressure is being logged and the elements were changed in March.

Delivery hoses are the greatest single weakness at this site. Of the eight hoses in service:

- Three are within date and in good condition.
- Two are within date but show cover chafing at the coupling ferrule, one of them severe enough that the reinforcement braid is visible.
- Two are out of test date, one by four months and one by eleven months.
- One has been repaired with a hose clamp and tape at the swaged end. This is not an acceptable repair and the hose was withdrawn on the spot and destroyed.

Hose test records are being kept in a notebook in the cabin rather than on the asset record. The two out of date hoses are to be withdrawn until tested, and the two chafed hoses are to be replaced. A holding of four spare hoses is recommended, which the site does not currently have.

## 4. Bunding

The bund is formed of earth banks with a welded liner. Required capacity is 110 per cent of the largest single container, and the bund as built is comfortably above that at about 34,000 litres, so capacity is not the issue.

The issue is integrity. The liner is punctured in two places on the eastern bank where a stake has been driven through it, apparently to secure a tarpaulin, and has a 400 mm tear at the north-west corner where plant has driven over the bank. The bund is therefore not a bund. Rainwater has been draining out through the tear rather than being pumped out, which is why nobody noticed.

The bund penetration for the suction line is sealed with mastic that has hardened and cracked and needs to be reformed with a proper puddle flange.

The bund emptying pump is present, serviceable and interlocked to a manual key so that it cannot be run unattended. The interlock is being observed.

## 5. Earthing and bonding

The site earth bar reads 4 ohms to earth, which is satisfactory.

Continuity from the earth bar was proven to the pump set, the filter housing, both dispensing points and four of the six bladder frames. It could not be proven at bladder frames 2 and 6; in both cases the bonding lead is present but the crimp at the frame end has corroded through. Both are to be remade and retested.

Both dispensing points have earthing reels for vehicle bonding. The reel at point 2 has a broken spring and the lead hangs in the dirt; the clip is corroded and does not bite. It is to be replaced. Bonding is being carried out by the operators, which is the important thing, but the equipment is not making it easy for them.

## 6. Throughput

Delivery rates were measured at each point into a calibrated vessel:

| Point | Rated | Measured, single point running | Measured, both points running |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dispensing point 1 | 450 l/min | 442 l/min | 394 l/min |
| Dispensing point 2 | 450 l/min | 415 l/min | 355 l/min |

Aggregate throughput with both points running is 749 l/min against a design 900 l/min. The shortfall is consistent with filter loading and with the restriction in the point 2 hose run. It is not a pump defect; suction and discharge pressures were normal.

The site is asked for a maximum of about 40,000 litres in a heavy day, which at the measured aggregate rate is under two hours of pumping. Capacity is not constraining the node and will not become constraining unless the demand roughly doubles.
