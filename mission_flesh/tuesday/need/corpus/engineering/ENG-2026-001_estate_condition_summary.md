## 1. Basis of the summary

This summary consolidates the inspection record for the eight assets on the estate register that carry a formal inspection interval. It draws on eleven inspections and surveys completed during 2025, together with the routine defect returns from each node. Where an asset has not been inspected inside its interval this is stated. No new asset has been added to the register during the period and none has been struck off.

Condition is scored A to E on the standing estate scale: A, as new or fully serviceable with no defects; B, serviceable with minor defects not affecting function; C, serviceable with defects that will affect function if untreated; D, function impaired, repair required; E, unserviceable. Criticality is scored 1 to 3 against the loss of capability that would follow failure of the asset: 1, corridor or station capability lost; 2, capability degraded and worked around at cost; 3, local inconvenience only. Spend priority is a single ranked ordering across the estate for the 2026 works allocation and is not a restatement of criticality; a highly critical asset in good condition ranks low.

## 3. Commentary

**Checkpoint 2 (W-19)** is the estate's first call on funds. Both barrier actuators have exceeded or are approaching the manufacturer's cycle life and neither has had a seal set replaced. The search bay surfacing has failed in the wheel tracks and the bay lighting is below the level a search task requires, with two heads out at the time of survey. A phased actuator replacement was costed at ENG-2025-031 and should be let early in the year. Works planning at this site is straightforward in one respect: under MEM-2026-041 Checkpoint 2 passes no traffic between 2200Z and 0500Z and AMBER is worked as a daylight corridor, so the overnight period is available to Estate Works without traffic management.

**Marrow Cut** carries the same criticality and only ranks second because the design is less advanced. Water stands at the low chainage after any sustained rain and the formation is pumping fines through the surface. A scheme is being drawn up and will be issued separately.

**Ferry Point (W-22).** The south slip is serviceable and needs nothing beyond routine sweeping and the annual check of the tie downs. The north slip shows spalling to the lower apron with reinforcement exposed over a limited area. A repair specification is in preparation. Work here is constrained by water level rather than by traffic and can only be prosecuted in short spells when the river is down; the specification will make that explicit. MV Tolland Carrier continues to work the south slip and is not affected.

**Node D fuel point (W-07)** is adequate for the throughput it is asked to deliver. The defects are in the secondary containment, where the bund liner has been punctured, and in the earthing installation, where continuity between the bladder frames and the earth bar is not proven at two points. Neither is expensive. Both are the sort of thing that becomes serious if left through another year.

**Bastion Reach (W-31)** has the worst surface on the estate and the lowest criticality; vehicles can be parked on the adjacent hardstanding at the cost of a longer walk. It is ranked fifth because the cost of doing nothing continues to rise as the sub base breaks up.

**Node B mast (W-04)** is in good structural order. The 2025 urgent list, which was mostly lighting and small ironmongery, was cleared in May. The mast needs repainting in the next two to three years and this should be programmed rather than allowed to arrive as a defect.

**Station Halberd main gate and perimeter** carries a long list of small items rather than any single serious one. The perimeter fence has been breached by fallen timber in two places and temporarily repaired with wire.

**Selwyn Bridge (W-41)** carries Route AMBER over the Selwyn at its rating of 40 tonnes. Structural works are outstanding and await call-off with Ardwick Structural. The asset is ranked last for the 2026 allocation because the works sit under a separate contract instruction and are not funded from the estate line.

## 4. Recommendation

That the 2026 estate allocation be applied in the priority order at section 2, that the Checkpoint 2 actuator programme and the Marrow Cut drainage scheme be let as the first two instructions of the year, and that a provision of not less than fifteen per cent of the allocation be held unallocated against defects arising.
