# Retaining Wall RW-3, Route CINDER - Movement and Monitoring Regime

**Reference:** ENG-2026-022
**Issued:** 11 May 2026
**Prepared by:** Voss, R. (Estate Works)
**Reviewed by:** Jankowski, D., Ardwick Structural
**Structure:** RW-3, mass concrete retaining wall, Route CINDER chainage 8+390 to 8+435
**Distribution:** Estate Works file; Movement Desk (Iyer, S.); node commander W-07; Ardwick Structural (Fitzhugh, A.) for record

**Classification:** TRAINING SYNTHETIC - EXERCISE NORTHWIND SHELF. All units, organisations, people, places and events in this document are invented.

## 1. The structure

RW-3 is a mass concrete gravity wall of 45 m length retaining the uphill side of Route CINDER where the road is cut into the valley side. Exposed height varies from 2.1 m at the northern end to 4.6 m at the deepest point near chainage 8+405. There is a stone masonry parapet above the wall for the middle 30 m. The wall is thought to be of the same construction period as the road and no design or construction records exist for it.

Drainage is by weepholes at approximately 3 m centres in a single row roughly 1 m above road level. Of the fifteen weepholes, six run, four are damp, and five are dry and appear blocked.

## 2. Observed movement

Outward movement at the crest was first recorded in the routine inspection of October 2024 as a 12 mm offset from a taut string line, and was recorded again in November 2025 at 18 mm. Neither reading was taken by a method good enough to be trusted to better than a few millimetres, but the direction and the trend are consistent.

The associated signs are:

- A vertical crack through the wall face at 8+402, presently about 6 mm wide at the crest and closing to hairline at road level. There is a small step of about 3 mm across the crack, the southern side standing proud.
- A second, tighter vertical crack at 8+417, about 2 mm.
- Displacement of two parapet copings above 8+402, one rocking under hand pressure.
- Longitudinal cracking in the carriageway surfacing 1.2 m out from the wall face, over about 9 m, which is the classic signature of the road following the wall out.
- Ground behind the wall is soft and there is a small area of standing water about 4 m back from the crest.

Nothing observed suggests a rapid or imminent failure. The pattern is of slow outward rotation, most likely driven by water pressure behind a wall with a partly blocked drainage system. That is a condition that can persist for years and can also accelerate quickly after heavy rain, and it will not be safe to assume the former.

## 3. Immediate works, already instructed

3.1 Rod and clear all fifteen weepholes and re-drill any that cannot be cleared. This is the single most useful thing that can be done and is instructed under HAL/WO/2026/112.

3.2 Fill and regrade the depression behind the crest so that surface water runs away from the wall rather than into the backfill.

3.3 Take down and rebed the two loose copings.

3.4 Set out and install the monitoring described below.

## 4. Monitoring regime

**Instrumentation.** Nine survey targets on the wall face in three columns of three, at the northern end, at 8+402 and at 8+417, fixed at crest, mid height and 500 mm above road level. Three additional targets on the parapet. Two reference stations established on rock outcrop clear of the influenced ground, one at each end. Two glass tell-tales and one vernier crack monitor across the crack at 8+402, and one vernier monitor at 8+417.

**Method.** Total station observation of all twelve targets from both reference stations, closing between them. Precision required, plus or minus 1 mm in plan and level at the ninety five per cent confidence level. Crack monitors read by hand at each visit and photographed. Observations to be by the same instrument and, so far as possible, the same observer.

**Baseline.** Two full sets of observations one week apart. The mean of the two is the zero.

**Frequency.** Monthly for the first six months. Thereafter quarterly if all readings are in the green band. An additional set of observations is to be taken within forty eight hours of any period of prolonged or heavy rain, and within twenty four hours of any report of new cracking, new surfacing distress, or debris on the carriageway at this location.

**Reporting.** A single sheet returned to Estate Works within three working days of each visit, giving the readings, the movement since baseline, the movement since the previous visit, and the band. Any amber or red reading is to be telephoned to Estate Works on the day it is taken and not left to the return.

## 5. Targets and trigger levels

Movement is measured as horizontal displacement of the crest targets, outward positive, from baseline. The rate figure is taken over the preceding thirty days.

| Band | Cumulative movement from baseline | Rate | Crack width at 8+402 | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Green | Up to 5 mm | Up to 1 mm per month | Up to 8 mm | Continue regime. No action. |
| Amber | Over 5 mm and up to 15 mm | Over 1 and up to 3 mm per month | Over 8 and up to 12 mm | Revert to monthly, or weekly if already monthly. Notify Movement Desk. Structural engineer to visit within ten working days. Begin design of a permanent remedial scheme. |
| Red | Over 15 mm | Over 3 mm per month | Over 12 mm | Notify Movement Desk immediately. Close the nearside lane and impose single file working past the structure under manual control. Structural engineer to attend within twenty four hours. Install temporary propping or unload the retained ground as directed. Daily observation until the trend is understood. |

Any single reading that jumps by more than 4 mm from the previous visit is to be treated as red until it has been proved to be an observation error by immediate re-observation, whatever the cumulative figure says. Two consecutive amber returns are to be treated as red.

The target for the structure is that after the weepholes are cleared and the ground behind the crest is regraded, movement settles into the green band and stays there. If it has not done so within six months of those works, the wall will need a permanent remedial scheme, most likely a row of drilled drains and either a toe berm or an anchored solution, and that should be budgeted for now rather than found later.

## 6. Traffic

No restriction is imposed at this time. The wall is on the uphill side and a failure would deposit material on the carriageway rather than remove support from it, which is the more forgiving of the two cases. Route CINDER remains open without qualification. The red band actions above are the point at which that changes.
