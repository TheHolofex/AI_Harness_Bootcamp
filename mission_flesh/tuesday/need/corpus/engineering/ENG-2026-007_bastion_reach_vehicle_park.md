## Existing condition

The vehicle park is a bituminous surface of about 2,750 square metres laid over an unbound sub base of unknown thickness, with a shallow fall to a single gully in the south-west corner. It has had no planned maintenance since it was laid.

The surface has failed over roughly half its area. There is extensive crocodile cracking in the bays nearest the entrance, ravelling across the central aisle, and seven potholes deeper than 75 mm, of which two expose the sub base. Where the sub base is exposed it is wet and contaminated with fines. Standing water covers about 90 square metres along the northern edge after rain because the ground has settled against the kerb line and the fall now runs the wrong way over the last 12 m.

The single gully is blind. Rodding in July found the connection choked with silt over its first 4 m and the outfall could not be traced beyond the boundary. The kerb line on the eastern side is displaced over 20 m where reversing vehicles have overrun it.

## Proposal

1. Trace and prove the existing outfall. If it cannot be proved, form a new soakaway in the open ground east of the park; the ground investigation for the 2024 hut foundations found free draining gravel at shallow depth and a soakaway is likely to work here.
2. Replace the single gully with three gullies, one at the existing position and two new along the northern edge, connected by 150 mm pipework to the proved outfall or the new soakaway.
3. Plane off the existing surface to 60 mm across the whole area.
4. Excavate and reconstruct the two areas of exposed and contaminated sub base, about 130 square metres in total, in full depth.
5. Regulate to restore a consistent fall of 2 per cent to the drainage line, correcting the reversed fall at the northern edge.
6. Lay 60 mm dense bitumen macadam surface course over the whole area.
7. Reset 20 m of displaced kerb and install six precast wheel stops at the eastern bays.
8. Remark the bays. The present layout gives 31 bays; the remarking is to give 27 slightly wider bays, which reflects how the park is actually used and will reduce the kerb overrun.
