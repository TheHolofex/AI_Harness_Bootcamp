## 1. The problem

The Marrow Cut runs for roughly 900 m between rock faces on both sides. Over the middle 460 m the road sits in a shallow sag, with the low point at about chainage 17+410. There is no positive drainage through the sag. Such surface water as reaches the edges either soaks away into the verge or stands.

Water stands across the full carriageway width at the low point after any sustained rain and takes between one and three days to clear, longer in cold weather. The consequences seen in the November inspection are:

- Fines are being pumped up through the surface at the low point over an area of about 60 square metres, with the characteristic grey staining and loss of surface texture.
- Two crescent cracks have opened in the nearside wheel track at 17+395 and 17+425.
- The verge on the east side has softened to the point that it will not take a vehicle wheel, reducing effective width for passing.
- Ice forms across the sag in cold conditions and the site has been gritted on eleven occasions since October.

Trial pits at four positions found the formation to be a silty sand with a perched water table within 400 mm of the surface at the low point, and drier at both ends of the sag. There is no evidence of a spring; the water is surface water with nowhere to go.

## 2. Scheme

The scheme is a conventional one and consists of three elements.

**Catchpits and connecting carrier drain.** Six catchpits, 450 mm diameter with 300 mm sumps, set in the east verge at 17+200, 17+290, 17+370, 17+440, 17+530 and 17+620, connected by a 225 mm carrier drain laid to fall northward and discharged to the existing rock cut channel at 17+660. Gully gratings to be the heavy duty pattern; the light pattern already in stock is not suitable for the traffic.

**French drain.** A 300 mm wide filter drain in the east verge running the full 460 m of the sag, formed with a 150 mm perforated pipe on a bedding layer, single sized clean stone surround, geotextile wrap to all faces and a sealed top to prevent silting from the verge. The French drain is to be connected into each catchpit rather than run past it, so that the system can be rodded pit to pit.

**Regrading.** The carriageway crossfall through the sag is presently between nil and one per cent and reverses twice. It is to be regraded to a consistent 2.5 per cent falling east by a regulating course of dense bitumen macadam, with a 40 mm surface course over the full 460 m. The two cracked areas at 17+395 and 17+425 are to be cut out to formation, the formation inspected, and reinstated in full depth before the regulating course goes down.

| 4 to 5 | Catchpits and carrier drain, south half |
| 6 to 7 | French drain, full length, connected pit to pit |
| 8 | Full depth reinstatement at 17+395 and 17+425 |
| 9 | Regulating course |
| 10 | Surface course and markings |
| 11 | Snagging, records, remove traffic management |

Eleven weeks from instruction, assuming a five day working week and no more than four days lost to weather. The surfacing operations at weeks 9 and 10 need dry conditions and should not be started against a poor forecast; if they slip, everything else can be completed and the sag left drained but unsurfaced without loss.

## 5. Traffic during the works

The Cut cannot be made single file for the full length; there is nowhere to pass. Works are to be organised in sections of not more than 120 m with a shuttle working under manual control and a marshal at each end in radio contact. Route AMBER is worked as a daylight corridor, Checkpoint 2 passing no traffic between 2200Z and 0500Z under MEM-2026-041, and the Movement Desk has confirmed that the works may take the carriageway outside those hours provided it is left trafficable each morning. No overnight lane occupation is proposed in any case.
