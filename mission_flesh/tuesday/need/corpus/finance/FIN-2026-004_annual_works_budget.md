## 1. Purpose and authority

1.1 This paper notifies the works allocation for the 2026 works year and apportions it by asset. It is issued under the delegation held by Finance for the apportionment of the estate maintenance and minor works vote. The Estate Works Board programmes schemes within the apportionment; Finance does not choose schemes.

1.2 The works year runs from 1 February 2026 to 31 January 2027.

## 2. Allocation

2.1 The allocation for the year is **210,000 exercise credits**. This is an increase of 14,000 credits, or 7.1 per cent, on the 2025 allocation of 196,000 credits.

2.3 The apportionment above is indicative rather than binding at asset level. The Estate Works Board may programme across assets provided the total commitment does not exceed the programmable allocation. Where a scheme is programmed at an asset materially in excess of its indicative apportionment, the Board is to record the fact in its minutes.

2.4 **Programmable allocation is 200,000 credits.** The contingency of 10,000 credits is held unallocated and is not available for programming at the start of the year. It may be released in whole or in part on the authority of the Chair of the Estate Works Board with Finance countersignature. Finance advises against release before the first quarter has run.

## 3. Basis of the apportionment

3.1 The apportionment was built from three inputs, weighted as shown.

| Input | Weight | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Condition survey scores at each asset | 50% | Estate condition survey, October 2025 |
| Three-year average outturn at each asset | 30% | Finance records |
| Forward requirement notified by the Estate Manager | 20% | Estate Manager's return, 8 January 2026 |

3.2 The two material movements against 2025 are explained as follows.

(a) **Node D, up 11,000 credits.** The October 2025 condition survey scored the hardstanding at Node D worse than any other trafficked surface on the estate, and the score had fallen two bands in a single year. The asset also carries the highest forward requirement in the Estate Manager's return.

(b) **Station Halberd, down 6,000 credits.** Outturn at Station Halberd in 2024 and 2025 was inflated by two one-off schemes that are now complete. The underlying requirement is lower, notwithstanding the accommodation block roof discussed at paragraph 4.2.

## 4. Commentary on pressures

4.1 Finance draws the Board's attention to the following pressures on the allocation. They are set out so that the Board programmes with its eyes open, not to direct any particular outcome.

4.2 **The accommodation block roof.** The largest single item in the Estate Manager's forward requirement is renewal of the roof covering on the accommodation block at Station Halberd, estimated at 61,000 credits. That is 30.5 per cent of the programmable allocation for one scheme at one asset. There have been three water ingress reports over the winter, all in the same bay, and Welfare supports the scheme. Finance offers no view on the merits but observes that if the scheme is taken in full, the Board will have between 130,000 and 140,000 credits for everything else on the estate, which will not cover the remainder of the forward requirement. The Board may wish to seek a partial scheme covering the affected bay only.

4.3 **Material cost movement.** Rates under the measured term arrangement have risen by between 5 and 9 per cent across the trades since the arrangement was let, with the sharpest movement in aggregates and in fencing materials. The 7.1 per cent uplift in the allocation is therefore close to neutral in real terms. The Board should not plan on the basis that a larger allocation buys more work.

4.4 **The term arrangement re-let.** The measured term arrangement expires in November 2026. Finance has made no provision for a rate increase on re-let, and any increase will fall on the final quarter of the works year. The Board may wish to front-load rather than back-load its programme.

4.5 **Deterioration at trafficked surfaces.** The condition survey records a general worsening of trafficked surfaces at Node D, Ferry Point and Bastion Reach. Where a surface has failed at formation rather than in the surface course, tenders can be expected to exceed estimates built on a surface-only assumption. Finance advises the Board to expect at least one tender in the year to come in materially over estimate and to hold enough uncommitted allocation to deal with it without abandoning a scheme.

4.6 **Small works.** The small works budget, which sits outside this allocation, is under strain. Several items that would once have been programmed are now being absorbed as small works, including signage, marking, timeclocks, fencing repairs and similar. Finance will report separately if the small works budget requires supplementation. The Board should not treat small works as a route around the programme.

4.7 **Items outside this allocation.** For the avoidance of doubt, the following do not fall on this allocation and are not to be programmed against it:

(a) structural works to the Selwyn Bridge (W-41), which are contracted with Ardwick Structural against a separate vote and which remain outstanding and awaiting call-off. Approach signage, barriers, markings and drainage on the approaches do fall on this allocation;

(b) the vehicle ferry charter at Ferry Point, which is an operating cost carried by the Movement Desk;

(c) hire of hardstanding, plant or accommodation, which are operating costs;

(d) fuel installations of a capital nature, which are the subject of a separate submission.

## 5. Rules of use

5.1 **Virement.** Movement of funds between programmed schemes requires a written virement request from the Estate Manager, approval by the Estate Works Board and countersignature by Finance. Virements are to be recorded in the Board minutes with the amount and both scheme references.

5.2 **Referral.** Any single commitment above 25,000 credits requires Finance sight of the tender report before placement.

5.3 **Carry forward.** Unspent allocation does not carry forward into the next works year. The Board should aim to commit its programme by the end of the third quarter.

5.4 **Reporting.** Finance will report commitment and outturn against this allocation to each meeting of the Estate Works Board.

## 6. Recommendation

6.1 The Estate Works Board is invited to note the allocation, to programme within the programmable allocation of 200,000 credits, and to have regard to the pressures at Section 4, in particular the treatment of the accommodation block roof at paragraph 4.2 and the advice at paragraph 4.5 on tender risk.
