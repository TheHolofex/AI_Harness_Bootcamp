## 1. Instruction

1.1 At its meeting of 16 April 2026 the Route Working Group referred to Finance the question whether the hire agreement for hardstanding at Node D should continue on its present terms, and directed a written review with a recommendation. This paper discharges that action.

1.2 The review covers the charging basis, measured usage, the cost the charge recovers, and the options open to the Group. It does not cover the operational layout of the site, which is a matter for the Estate Works Board and the Movement Desk.

## 4. Measured usage

4.1 Occupancy is recorded by the Node D detachment at a single daily observation, taken at 0900Z. This is a crude measure and understates use by vehicles that arrive and depart within the day, but it is the only record held.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| October 2025 | 11.2 | 16 | 0 |
| November 2025 | 10.4 | 15 | 0 |
| December 2025 | 8.9 | 13 | 0 |
| January 2026 | 9.6 | 14 | 0 |
| February 2026 | 10.1 | 15 | 0 |
| March 2026 | 12.3 | 17 | 0 |
| April 2026 | 13.5 | 17 | 0 |
| May 2026 | 14.1 | 18 | 1 |
| **Mean over period** | **11.3** | | |

4.2 Two features are worth drawing out.

(a) Mean occupancy over the period is 11.3 bays against 18 licensed, or 63 per cent. On that measure the consortium pays for some six and a half bays a month that it does not, at the observation point, occupy. This is the substance of the consortium's informal complaint reported at MIN-2026-04-16/RWG, Item 6.4.

(b) The trend is firmly upward. Occupancy in May was 14.1 against 8.9 in December, and the licensed bay count was reached for the first time in May. If the trend continues the agreement will be fully used by the autumn, and the complaint will disappear of its own accord. Finance would be reluctant to restructure a charge in response to a position that is already correcting.

4.3 **Idle bays are not available to MERIDIAN.** Licensed bays cannot be used by MERIDIAN vehicles even when empty. The Group should therefore not think of the unoccupied bays as spare capacity. They are capacity MERIDIAN has sold.

4.4 **Restricted bays.** Bays 24 to 31 at Node D are restricted to light vehicles pending the hardstanding repair scheme. Four of the eighteen licensed bays fall within that range. The consortium is therefore paying the full rate for four bays that it cannot use for its heavier vehicles, through no fault of its own and because of a defect in an asset that MERIDIAN is obliged to repair. Finance regards this as the strongest point the consortium has, and it is a point about repair rather than about the charging basis.

## 5. What the charge recovers

5.1 The rate of 340 credits per bay per month was set in September 2025 by reference to hire rates then quoted for comparable hardstanding in the district. It was not built up from cost. For context, the attributable cost of the facility is estimated as follows.

| Element | Annual, credits | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Annualised construction cost | 13,000 | 195,000 over a fifteen-year life |
| Surface maintenance | 9,300 | Three-year average, all bays |
| Site services (lighting, drainage, security, sweeping) | 40,000 | Node D site cost apportioned to the hardstanding |
| **Total attributable cost, 31 bays** | **62,300** | |
| Per bay per year | 2,010 | |
| Per bay per month | 167 | |

5.2 The charge is therefore roughly double attributable cost. That is not improper for a commercial licence, and the comparator evidence supports the rate, but the Group should be aware of the margin if the consortium chooses to press the point. It also means there is room to make a concession without going below cost.

## 6. Options

6.1 **Option A - do nothing.** Serve no notice; the agreement rolls on unchanged. Income certain at 73,440 credits a year. Leaves the restricted bay grievance unanswered and risks the consortium serving its own notice.

6.2 **Option B - move to a usage basis.** Charge for bays occupied at the daily observation. On the measured figures this would have yielded some 46,104 credits over twelve months against 73,440, a reduction of 27,336 credits. It also transfers the risk of idle bays from the consortium to MERIDIAN while MERIDIAN retains the repair obligation, and it makes income unforecastable. Finance does not recommend it. If it were adopted it should carry a floor of not fewer than twelve chargeable bays a month.

6.3 **Option C - reduce the licensed bay count.** Reduce from eighteen bays to fourteen, releasing four bays to MERIDIAN. Income falls to 4,760 credits a month, or 57,120 a year. This has the merit of matching the licence to observed use, but it is the wrong direction given the trend at paragraph 4.2(b), and MERIDIAN would probably be asked to license the bays back within the year at a rate it could not then set unilaterally.

6.4 **Option D - retain the fixed charge with a temporary concession on restricted bays.** Serve no notice. Charge the four licensed bays within the restricted range at half rate, that is 170 credits per bay per month, from 1 July 2026 until the hardstanding repair is complete and the restriction lifted. All other terms unchanged.

| Line | Credits |
| --- | --- |

| Four restricted bays at 170 | 680 per month |
| Monthly income during the concession | 5,440 |
| Monthly reduction against present terms | 680 |
| Estimated total reduction, assuming a three-month concession | 2,040 |

## 7. Recommendation

7.1 Finance recommends **Option D**.

7.2 The reasons are these. The consortium's real grievance is that it is paying full rate for bays MERIDIAN cannot presently offer in full condition, and that grievance is answerable cheaply and honestly. The wider complaint about paying for unoccupied bays is a complaint about the nature of a licence rather than about this licence, and it is in any event being overtaken by rising use. A move to a usage basis would cost materially more than the concession, would put an unforecastable line into the budget, and would leave MERIDIAN carrying both the repair obligation and the occupancy risk. The concession costs an estimated 2,040 credits, ends automatically when the repair is done, and requires no change to the charging structure.

7.3 The Group is invited to:

(a) agree that no notice be served under HHA-2025-004, so that the agreement rolls on from 3 September 2026;

(b) approve the temporary half-rate concession on the four licensed bays within the restricted range, effective 1 July 2026 and ending on completion of the hardstanding repair;

(c) direct Contracts to record the concession by exchange of letters rather than by formal variation;

(d) note that the daily observation record is a poor measure of use and direct that a better record be kept before the next renewal, whatever charging basis applies.

7.4 For the avoidance of doubt, this review does not touch the separate monthly contribution paid by the consortium towards the vehicle ferry charter at Ferry Point, which is made under a side letter of 14 November 2025 and is unaffected.
