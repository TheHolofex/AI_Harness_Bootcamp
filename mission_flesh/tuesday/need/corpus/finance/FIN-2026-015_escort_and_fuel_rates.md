## 1. Why this paper is issued

1.1 Finance has had four queries in six weeks about which escort and fuel rates are current, and one estimate was submitted using a rate withdrawn in 2025. This paper restates the rates in force. It introduces no change.

1.2 The rates below were set on 12 January 2026 under FIN-2026-002. They remain in force. No in-year revision is proposed and none is under consideration. The next scheduled revision is January 2027.

## 2. Escort rates

2.1 The following rates are used for planning and estimating escort tasks.

| Element | Rate | Unit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Escort crew, per person | 47 | credits per hour |
| Escort vehicle | 34 | credits per hour |
| Overhead uplift | 12 | per cent, applied to the sum of the above |
| Standard escort detail (one vehicle, two crew), all in | 143 | credits per hour |
| Enhanced escort detail (two vehicles, four crew), all in | 287 | credits per hour |
| Minimum charge, any escort task | 4 | hours |

2.2 **Derivation.** The crew rate is taken from the pay band tables applicable to the ranks routinely found for escort duty, weighted by the actual mix of ranks employed on escort tasks in the twelve months to 31 October 2025, and expressed as an hourly cost including employer on-costs. The vehicle rate is taken from fleet running cost records for the same period, comprising fuel, tyres, servicing, repair and an annualised depreciation charge, divided by recorded running hours rather than by distance, because escort vehicles spend a substantial proportion of their tasked time stationary. The overhead uplift of 12 per cent covers tasking, communications, administration and the share of squadron overhead that Finance judges attributable to escort work. It is a standing factor and is not recalculated task by task.

2.3 **Time counted.** Escort time runs from the escort's departure from its parent location to its return to that location, including time held at a start point, at a checkpoint or at a turn-round point. Time spent by an escort laid up overnight is counted at the crew rate only for hours actually on duty, and the vehicle rate is not charged for hours the vehicle is parked.

2.4 **Minimum charge.** Any escort task attracts a minimum of four hours whatever its actual duration. This reflects the fact that a crew and vehicle committed to a short task cannot in practice be employed on anything else that day.

## 3. Fuel rates

3.1 The planning price for fuel is **1.42 credits per litre**.

3.2 **Derivation.** The price is the volume-weighted average of the four quarterly bulk delivery invoices to 31 December 2025, plus 4 per cent to cover handling, storage losses and the cost of forward holding at detachments. It is a planning price and will not match any individual invoice.

3.3 **Consumption planning factors.** Where actual consumption records are not available, estimates are to use the following.

| Vehicle class | Litres per 100 km |
| --- | --- |
| Light utility | 14 |
| Medium load carrier | 29 |
| Heavy load carrier | 44 |
| Bulk fuel vehicle | 41 |
| Plant transporter | 52 |

3.4 **Adjustments.**

(a) A winter uplift of 6 per cent is applied to the consumption factors for the months of November to March inclusive.

(b) An idling allowance of 2.5 litres per hour is applied for any period a vehicle is planned to be stationary with the engine running, including at holding areas and at controlled crossings.

(c) No uplift is applied for terrain or for load state. The factors above already reflect the mix of conditions actually encountered.

## 4. How the rates are to be used

4.1 These rates are for planning, estimating and internal comparison. They are not to be used for:

(a) recharging to a third party. Recharges to the Cordell Basin Relief Consortium or to any other partner are raised at actual cost with supporting records, on the basis set out in the relevant agreement;

(b) insurance or loss claims, which are settled on actual and evidenced cost;

(c) any submission for additional funds, which must be supported by quotation or by actual outturn.

4.2 **Rounding.** Round to the nearest whole credit at the level of the serial, not at the level of each line. Do not carry fractions of a credit through a calculation and then round at the end; it invites arithmetic that cannot be checked.

4.3 **Records.** Where actual consumption or actual escort hours are recorded, use the actuals. The planning factors exist because records are sometimes absent, not because they are better than records.

## 5. Queries

5.1 Queries on these rates are to come to Finance in writing. Finance will not give a rate over the telephone.

5.2 Any estimate submitted using a rate other than those above will be returned.
