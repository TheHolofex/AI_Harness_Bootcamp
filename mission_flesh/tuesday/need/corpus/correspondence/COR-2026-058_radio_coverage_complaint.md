# Radio Coverage on the CINDER Corridor: Request for a Survey

Reference: COR-2026-058
Sent: 21 July 2026, 1725Z
From: Detachment Commander, Node D (W-07)
To: Achebe, N. (Signals), Task Group MERIDIAN, Station Halberd
Copy to: Chief of Staff; Iyer, S. (Movement Desk); Detachment Commander, Ferry Point (W-22)
Filed by: Signals registry, MERIDIAN

**Classification:** TRAINING SYNTHETIC - EXERCISE NORTHWIND SHELF. All units, organisations, people, places and events in this document are invented.

Mr Achebe,

1. I am formally requesting a radio coverage survey of the CINDER corridor between
Node D and Ferry Point. I have raised this on the net, at two coordination
meetings and in a note to your cell in May. I am putting it in writing so that it
is on a file somewhere rather than in somebody's notebook.

2. **The problem.** There is a stretch of roughly four kilometres, from the crest
south of Node D down to the river flats, where a vehicle cannot raise either Node D
or Ferry Point reliably. Sometimes it works. More often it does not, and the driver
gets nothing at all rather than a degraded signal. The worst of it is in the cutting
before the flats, where I would expect trouble, but it is also bad on open ground
south of the crest, where I would not.

3. **What we have observed.**

| Location | Effect | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Crest south of Node D, both directions | Intermittent. Node D readable, Ferry Point not. | Most days |
| The cutting, chainage 3+100 to 4+400 | Nothing either way | Reliably, every time |
| River flats, north of the ferry approach | Ferry Point readable, Node D not | Most days |
| Ferry marshalling area | Good | Always |
| Node D vehicle parks | Good | Always |

4. This is not a set of measurements. It is what my drivers and my watchkeeper have
written in the occurrence book over about three months, and I offer it as the
reason for a survey rather than as a substitute for one.

5. **Why it matters.** A vehicle on CINDER between the two locations is out of
contact for a substantial part of its run. If it breaks down, has an accident, or
is turned back at the slipway because the service has been lifted, we find out when
it arrives somewhere or when it does not. On 9 July a vehicle sat at the ferry
approach for over an hour after a lift was called, because the marshal could not
reach us and our watchkeeper could not reach the driver. That is a nuisance. On a
bad night with an injured driver it would be more than a nuisance.

6. **What I am asking for.**

6.1 A coverage survey of the corridor, driven, at the frequencies we actually use,
in the vehicles we actually use, with the antennas we actually have fitted. I make
that point because the last assessment I am aware of was done on a map.

6.2 An assessment of whether a repeater on the crest would fix the cutting, or
whether the terrain makes that pointless.

6.3 If a repeater is not the answer, advice on what is. I am not qualified to
propose the solution and I am not trying to.

7. **What I have already tried.** Antenna checks on the four vehicles that run the
route most often, all serviceable. Relocation of the Node D base antenna to the
higher of the two masts, which made a small difference on the crest and none in the
cutting. A trial of a relay through Bastion Reach, which did not work and which my
signaller says was never likely to.

8. I understand your cell is stretched and that the mast work at Station Halberd
has taken your people for most of the summer. I also note that Lieutenant Colonel
Osei is visiting on 25 and 26 August specifically to travel the CINDER corridor and
look at coverage. If the survey cannot be done before then, I would ask that it be
done as part of that visit rather than deferred again afterwards, and that the
visit party travel the cutting rather than being told about it.

9. Ferry Point detachment supports this request and has its own occurrence book
entries which I have asked them to send you direct.

Detachment Commander
Node D (W-07)
