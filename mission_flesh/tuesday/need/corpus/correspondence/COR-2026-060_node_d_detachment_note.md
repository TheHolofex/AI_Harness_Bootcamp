# COR-2026-060 - Node D detachment, note on staging for the September lift

**Sent:** 2026-08-14
**From:** Node D detachment commander
**To:** Movement Desk; Supply Desk, Task Group MERIDIAN
**Filed by:** Movement Desk
**Classification:** TRAINING SYNTHETIC - EXERCISE NORTHWIND SHELF. All units, organisations, people, places and events in this document are invented.

Sending this unasked because I gather CINDER is being considered as the September
primary and I do not think the people doing the considering have been here.

We can stage a serial. We have staged serials. What we have never done is stage
serials back to back for two weeks, and I want to set out honestly what that
would look like from where I sit.

**Space.** The bound hardstanding takes a serial and no more. The survey says 47
vehicles and that is about right on a good day with a sensible mix. The gravel
area to the east is on the survey as capacity and I would not count it in
September. We have used it twice, both times in July, and both times we regraded
afterwards. Put a rotation lift across it after rain and we will be recovering
our own vehicles.

**Fuel.** Two hoses. Thirty vehicles is four hours. That is the number that will
bite, not the space. If a serial arrives at last light and has to be fuelled
before it stages, it is fuelled in the dark by torchlight, and I would rather it
was not.

**Circulation.** One way in, one way out, shared for the first stretch. If a
serial is arriving while another is leaving, one of them waits. In a sustained
lift that happens every day.

**The crossing.** I am not going to comment on the ferry because it is not mine
and the operator has told you their position in writing. What I will say is that
when the crossing stands down, the vehicles do not evaporate. They sit here. And
we do not have room for a serial that is waiting plus a serial that is arriving.
A stand-down of two consecutive days would put vehicles on the road outside the
gate, which is a Consortium road.

**What would help.** The circulation fix, which has been on the works list since
before I got here. A second fuel point. And a realistic assumption about how many
days in the window the crossing will actually work, which I do not have and which
nobody has given me.

I am not arguing for AMBER. I am arguing against anybody assuming Node D is a
bigger place than it is.
