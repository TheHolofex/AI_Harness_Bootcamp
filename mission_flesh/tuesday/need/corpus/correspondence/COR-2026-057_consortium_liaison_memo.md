# COR-2026-057 - Cordell Basin Relief Consortium, position on the September rotation

**Received:** 2026-08-10
**From:** Adeyemi, F., Field Coordinator, Cordell Basin Relief Consortium
**To:** Liaison Desk, Task Group MERIDIAN
**Filed by:** Liaison Desk
**Classification:** TRAINING SYNTHETIC - EXERCISE NORTHWIND SHELF. All units, organisations, people, places and events in this document are invented.

Thank you for asking for our view before the route decision. We are not a party
to it and we understand that, but our own movements share both corridors and you
asked, so here it is.

**We favour Route CINDER for the September rotation.**

Our reasons, in order of weight.

First, the northern corridor cannot be run at night. **Checkpoint 2 is shut
between 2200Z and 0500Z under your own MEM-2026-041**, which means every serial
on AMBER has to be squeezed into the same morning hours. In a rotation of this
size that is a queue, and our traffic sits in the same queue. The southern
corridor has no such restriction and can in principle be worked across a longer
day.

Second, AMBER puts everything across a single structure. If the Selwyn Bridge crossing
is lost for any reason the corridor is lost with it. The ferry crossing has the
same character but the consequence is different, because a ferry that stops
today runs tomorrow, and a bridge that is closed for works is closed for weeks.

Third, our own experience of the ferry is good. Our vehicles cross at Ferry Point
most weeks and we have found the operator reliable and the crossing quick. I
should be straight with you that our traffic is heaviest between May and August
and lightest in the autumn, so our experience is of the summer river rather than
the autumn one.

Fourth, the consortium's staging at Node D is already established and we would
make it available to you on the existing terms.

What we would say against CINDER, so that you have it from us rather than having
to find it: the corridor is a third longer, the ferry is a single point of
failure of a different kind, and the operator does stand the crossing down when
the river is up. We do not know how often that happens in September and we have
never had to plan around it.

I would rather you took the decision on your own figures than on our impressions.
