# Harness case talks — Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday AM

**When:** last **45 minutes before lunch** on Tue / Wed / Thu morning.  
**Shape:** **30 minutes** lead presentation · **15 minutes** open discussion.  
**No hard wall-clock gate** into lunch — if the room is deep in a useful thread, finish the thought; do not cut a real operator story for the schedule.

These are not product demos and not theory lectures. Each talk is one **real problem the lead solved with harnessed AI**, how the harness mattered, and what the experience felt like under judgment.

## Purpose

Students have spent the morning *inside* a capability (craft, knowledge, autonomy). Before lunch they hear how that same craft looks on a real desk — with scars, bounds, and evidence — then pressure-test it in discussion.

## Fixed shape (every talk)

| Minutes | What |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Name the **real-world problem** (who hurt, what was on fire, what “done” had to mean) |
| 3–10 | What you tried **without** a serious harness (or with a weak one) — and where it failed |
| 10–22 | How harness pieces changed the outcome (instructions, tools, tests, memory, second engine, contract, pipeline… **only the ones that actually moved the needle**) |
| 22–28 | Evidence you trusted · what you still owned as the human · one thing you’d do differently |
| 28–30 | One transfer question for the room |
| 30–45 | Discussion — students first; lead answers last |

## Day mapping (suggested theme, not a script)

| Morning | Block just run | Case-talk theme (pick a real story that fits) |
|---|---|---|
| **Tuesday** | P2 personal control plane | One real product made dependable by correctly placed instructions, skills, hooks, plugins, subagents, or MCP—only the controls that actually earned their runtime cost. Show the product, release receipt, component decision, and human judgment that remained. |
| **Wednesday** | P4 personal operating harness | One real goal carried by a bounded loop: why the control pattern fit, where durable state mattered, which human gate earned its interruption, what evidence ended the run, and which tempting component was left out or removed. |

You may swap stories if a better live example fits the room. Keep the **capability** of that morning’s block audible in the story.

## Discussion prompts (use 2–3, not all)

- What part of that story was **tool-enforced** vs **procedure-enforced**?
- Where would this have failed on *your* desk without a harness?
- What evidence would your supervisor accept — and what would they reject as vibe?
- What would you **not** automate next time, and why?
- One change this story makes to the morning module&#x27;s existing Transfer 30-60-90 seed. Amend that same row before lunch; do not create a second seed.

## Lead posture

- First person, concrete, scar-inclusive. Guide-beside energy, not keynote.
- Show an artifact when you can (brief, log line, score-sheet row, contract snippet, PR, ticket) — not a slide deck of abstractions.
- Name the harness parts in plain words when they appear; do not dump a glossary.
- Leave air in the 15 minutes. Silence after a hard question is useful.

## What this is not

- Not a second mission block (no PASS_BARS for the talk).
- Not vendor pitch time.
- Not “how I use ChatGPT for email.”
- Not optional for the lead — the room counts on the pattern Tue/Wed/Thu.

## Student expectation (one line)

Listen for the problem, the harness move, and the evidence — then spend discussion time making it legible to *your* work, not re-litigating the lead’s choices.
