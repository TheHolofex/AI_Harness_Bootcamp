Why this block exists

Capable systems attract bad intake: false citations, contradictions, hostile instructions. You will catch three classes mechanically and prove the trusted vault stayed clean.

What you will leave with

  • Define trusted boundary before intake
  • Catch false citation, field contradiction, hostile instruction with evidence
  • Prove absence of effect in trusted notes
  • Sandbox vs approvals named as separate controls; Codex app vs OpenCode bounds compared (one clear sentence each); an OpenCode rule set read and its effect predicted before running
  • Reusable intake rule; transfer P5 seed; pulse

Run of exercise

  1. Open the chair

    Open your Codex app project. Confirm operator templates are present (DIRECTION_BRIEF.md, OPERATOR_LOG.md, etc.).

    Open chat Operator — Direction & Log. Paste the Direction Brief starter from the operator pack.

  2. Co-write the Direction Brief until LIVE

    Work the five fields with the AI: Outcome, Done looks like, Bounds, Evidence standard, Stop/hand-back.

    Do not start the mission until status is LIVE. Vague “explore the data” outcomes get challenged — name the artifact.

  3. Run the mission

    Keep yesterday’s trusted vault. Intake pack: mission_flesh/p5/intake_poisoned/.

    Before connecting intake, freeze in your brief what “trusted” means and the quarantine path.

    1. Run intake through a guarded process (staging folder / triage note — not blind dump into trusted).
    2. Catch false citation, field contradiction, and hostile instruction with evidence (flags, quarantine files, failed checks).
    3. Prove trusted notes do not contain the hostile payload or false fact (absence of effect).
    4. Name what the Codex app enforces versus what stays procedure. Two controls do different jobs: the sandbox decides which files and network the agent can reach; approvals decide when it pauses. Switching from Ask for approval to Approve for me changes who reviews the escalation — it does not widen the sandbox. Say in one sentence which of your three catches a mode would have stopped, and which needed you.
    5. Compare against OpenCode, which writes bounds as rules in opencode.json rather than as a mode you pick in a menu. Its rules resolve last-match-wins: a later allow overrides an earlier deny, so the shipped defaults read *.env deny, then *.env.example allow, and the order is the whole meaning. Read a rule set and predict what it permits before you run anything against it. Then write one sentence on how the two tools express the same bound, and where enforcement is weaker or merely conventional.
    6. Write the intake rule you will reuse at work.

  4. Operator Log + pass-bar draft

    Back in Direction & Log, paste the log starter. Record verdict, evidence, what you still own.

    Self-check against PASS_BARS.md for P5 — met / not yet with pointers. No inflation.

  5. Adversarial review (new chat)

    Open a new chat titled Adversarial — P5. Paste the frozen prompt from the operator pack plus your artifacts and provisional verdict.

    Copy the final line into your log: ADVERSARIAL: stood | wounded | failed — …. Fix if wounded.

  6. Measurement spine

    Update MEASUREMENT_SPINE.md: ritual 0–3, mission HIT/PARTIAL/MISS, quality STRONG/ADEQUATE/WEAK, time to result (minutes).

  7. Transfer pulse

    Chat Operator — Transfer 30-60-90. Paste the every-session transfer prompt. Leave a session-log row even if the seed is small today.

Pass bar (mastery floor)

Full checklist: operator/PASS_BARS.md · section P5. MVP is not “it ran.” Evidence must survive adversarial review.

If you get stuck

  • Narrow data scope, not the judgment standard.
  • Re-read your LIVE brief — most chaos is an unbounded outcome.
  • Ask for a lead diagram of the one concept blocking you, then continue on your chair.
